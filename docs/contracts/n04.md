Bead `ffw-nx0`. Code: `crates/franken-stream/src/playout.rs` and `playout/log.rs` (tests in `playout/tests.rs` and
`playout/log/tests.rs`). Independent reference,

Status: **host simulation only, slice 1 and its diagnostics (section 15).** Nothing here touches a device, a network or a clock other than the ones the
tests construct. The Rust was written before it was compiled (this document and the Python side were checked on the host;
`rustfmt` parses the Rust, nothing more); the evidence level of every claim below is the judged central run recorded in the

## 1. What this is, and what it is not

The receive-side playout buffer for one stream: it holds admitted blocks until their cutoff, then takes exactly one decision
per block (real, concealed, muted or skipped), and reports it to the render loop.

* **A decision engine, not a render loop.** It reads no clock and drives no device. Every call carries a `ClockView`: the group
  time now, the clock's uncertainty, and whether the clock may be used. The caller builds it from N03 (`GroupClock::group_ns`,
  `ClockReport::uncertainty_ns`; usable while the status is Locked or Holdover). The seam is plain integers, and the engine has
  no dependency on the clock crate; a dev-only edge `franken-stream -> franken-clock` (section 14, item 3) lets
  `playout/group_clock_tests.rs` build every view from a real `GroupClock`. The uncertainty is an argument to every call,
  never a constant.
* **The sender's `consume_by_ns` header field is not used.** The sender cannot know this board's downstream cost, and a sender
  must not be able to extend a receiver's deadline. The receiver computes its own cutoff (section 3). `n04_the_senders_consume_by_never_extends_a_deadline`
  builds a packet that claims a deadline a second after presentation and shows the cutoff still governs.
* **Authenticity and replay are N01's job.** The engine assumes a packet came from `StreamReceiver::accept`; it re-checks what
  is left (epoch, window, timeline, duplicates, lateness). `MediaPacket::from_authenticated` is the adapter and drops `consume_by_ns`.
* **Fixed memory.** `horizon_blocks` slots of 1 KiB, allocated once in `PlayoutEngine::new`; with diagnostics on, `capacity` lines
  of 1 KiB more, allocated once in `enable_log` (section 15). The consume path (`on_packet`, `on_repair`, `decide`) uses no `Vec`,
  `Box` or `String` and copies at most one block per call; with diagnostics on it also formats integers and fixed words into the
  preallocated ring through `core::fmt`, which does not allocate. This is a structural claim, probed on the host by `N04-NO-ALLOC` (section 14, item 4).
* **Memory admission.** A slot is the 1024-byte payload, a `u16` frame count and a use flag: 1027 bytes, 1028 with the count's alignment
  (`SLOT_BYTES`). A profile's decoded playout queue is its `horizon_blocks` slots, at least one (`PlayoutProfile::playout_queue_bytes`:
  94 x 1028 = 96 632 bytes for `music`, 9 x 1028 = 9 252 for `realtime`), and the engine occupies that plus its own struct
  (`PlayoutEngine::resident_bytes`; the struct holds the 128-entry history, the 64 arrival margins and the last real block, so at least 1664
  bytes). `PlayoutEngine::new_within(profile, cfg, budget_bytes)` is the constructor for a caller that owns a memory ledger: the
  configuration is judged first, then the resident bytes against the budget, and a profile that does not fit is refused with a typed
  `CapacityError` (`needed_bytes`, `budget_bytes`) **before anything is allocated**. It substitutes no smaller profile and touches no
  existing engine, so a stream that is playing is never evicted to make room (bead, section 9). It admits exactly at the budget and
  refuses one byte short (`N04-CAPACITY`, section 12); section 14, item 11 says what it does not cover. Every step of the footprint is a
  checked operation (a product or sum that would wrap is a refusal, never a small number), and two compile-time assertions hold for every
  target the crate is built for: the slot is its fields rounded up to its alignment, and the widest profile a `u16` horizon can name fits a
  `usize`. The real media path is admitted the same way and through the same function: `MediaPlayout::new(receiver, profile, config, ledger, resident_elsewhere_bytes)` (franken-speaker) takes the platform's `MemoryBudgetLedger`, checks its whole-system floor (`verify_budget`), then fits the path inside the ledger's `stream_and_jitter_bytes` line **beside** the bytes the other resident paths hold (during a replacement, the playing path's `resident_bytes()`, so the old and the new count together at the peak), and returns `MediaAdmitError` (`Platform` or `Engine`, the engine's own refusal reasons unchanged) before anything is built; the virtual-house adapter builds its per-generation engines through the same `admit_engine`. A refusal touches no other path, and `VirtualSpeakerNode::switch_stream` is a transaction: it admits and opens the candidate engine first and only then rebinds the receiver and swaps the senders, so a refused source change leaves the old stream authenticating. A group source change is prepare-all-then-commit: `execute_source_switch` builds and admits every member's next engine against that member's own ledger before the arbiter, the program or any member identity is touched, reserves the program entry, then races at the arbiter and commits every member; a refusal at any member (or an error in the race) discards every candidate and leaves all of it as it was. The arbiter's own `submit` is not made transactional. The Virtual House campaign has one explicit expected-capacity-refusal scenario (`Scenario::expect_capacity_refusal`, test-only): at the source change the named later member's line is cut to old-plus-candidate-minus-one, only that typed refusal is accepted as a rejected control command (one refusal recorded, arbiter, program and every member unchanged) and the campaign carries on, comparing with the same-seed no-change run block for block and sample for sample; every other refusal, and a run in which the expected one does not happen, still fails the campaign.

## 2. Units and the timeline

Group-clock nanoseconds (`u64`), 48 kHz frames, blocks of 256 frames (N01 `start_frame` granularity).

* `frames_to_ns(f) = floor(f * 62500 / 3)`: exactly `floor(f * 1e9 / 48000)`. 256 frames is 5 333 333 ns (floor of 5 333 333.33), 48 000 frames is 1e9.
* A stream is bound by `begin_stream(epoch, anchor_frame, anchor_ns)`: `anchor_ns` is the group time at which `anchor_frame` is
  presented (N03's admitted `StartSchedule`). The presentation of frame `f` is `origin + frames_to_ns(f)` with
  `origin = anchor_ns - frames_to_ns(anchor_frame)`. `anchor_frame` must be a multiple of 256 and `anchor_ns` in
  `[frames_to_ns(anchor_frame), 2^62]`.
* **The engine derives every block's presentation time from this timeline** and treats a packet's header `presentation_time_ns`
  only as a consistency check within `tolerance_ns` (below half a block). So the cutoff of a block never depends on whether
  its packet arrived, and a sender cannot move one room's timeline by stamping packets differently (section 8).
* The same epoch may be bound again (`N03` re-admits a start within its epoch after a relock); an older one may not.

## 3. The cutoff

    consume_by(block) = presentation - work_ns - lead_ns - uncertainty_ns          (clamped at 0)

`work_ns` is the admitted downstream DSP and conversion work, `lead_ns` the device submission lead; **both must be positive**
(`PlayoutConfig::validate`: a zero cost is never true). `uncertainty_ns` comes from the `ClockView` of the call.

* **Boundary rule.** A packet or repair is on time iff its arrival is **strictly before** the cutoff. A decision is due iff the
  group time is at or after it. The two are complementary at equal views, so an arrival and a decision at the same instant
  cannot both claim the block: arrival at `c - 1` plays, at `c` and at `c + 1` is late (`N04-CUTOFF-BOUNDARY`).
* **Uncertainty moves the cutoff earlier by exactly its size**: at one arrival time, `u = 499 999` plays and `u = 500 000` is late
  (case `W03`). Each call is judged against the `u` it brings; nothing is stored, so a tighter clock restores a later cutoff.
* **Overdue.** A decision taken at or after the block's presentation time cannot be played: the block is dropped and the
  decision is `muted_overdue` (`t = presentation - 1` still plays, `t = presentation` is overdue: `W11B`). The render loop that
  stalls catches up one block per `decide` call, each block still decided exactly once.
* `Decision.submit_deadline_ns = presentation - lead_ns` and `late_by_ns = now - cutoff` are reported for every real or
  concealed block. A late recovered frame never rewinds the sample clock: the cursor only advances.

## 4. Admission (`on_packet`, `on_repair`)

Checks, in this order; each rejection is counted in its own slot and **changes nothing else**:

1. engine not bound -> `not_bound`; `epoch` below the stream's -> `stale_epoch`; above -> `future_epoch` (epochs arrive by control, never by media).
2. clock not usable -> `clock_unusable`; group time earlier than the last accepted view -> `clock_regression`. Nothing is stored and no block is consumed.
3. `start_frame` not a multiple of 256, `frames` outside `1..=256`, or `payload.len() != frames * 4` -> `bad_input`.
4. block already decided: a duplicate if the history says it was played, otherwise `late` (the history is 128 blocks).
5. block at or beyond `cursor + horizon_blocks` -> `too_far_ahead`; the window is **exactly** the slot count, so the queue cannot exceed its profile (`W08`).
6. slot already filled -> `duplicate` (the first copy wins; a copy with different bytes also bumps `duplicate_conflict`).
7. media only: header presentation time farther than `tolerance_ns` from the timeline -> `timeline_mismatch`.
8. arrival at or after the cutoff -> `late`. Otherwise stored: `admitted`.

A repair (`on_repair`) takes the same path minus step 7: **the same cutoff**. A repair after it is discarded even when the DAC
presentation time is still ahead (`W09B`), and a duplicate or late repair is counted apart from media.

## 5. The decision (`decide`)

When the next block's cutoff is reached, exactly one decision, then the cursor advances. In this order:

| situation | outcome |
|---|---|
| block is in a skip range of a latency decrease (section 8) | `skipped`, immediately |
| group time at or after presentation | `muted_overdue` |
| the block arrived in time | `real` (its bytes are in the caller's buffer) |
| nothing played yet | `muted_nohistory` |
| `max_conceal_blocks` consecutive blocks already not real | `muted_gap` |
| otherwise | `concealed{run}` |

**Concealment** is the last played block, every 16-bit sample multiplied by a Q15 gain for its run (`round(0.7^n * 32768)`,
n = 1..=8: 22938, 16056, 11239, 7868, 5507, 3855, 2699, 1889), rounded half up (`(s*g + 16384) >> 15`, floor shift). It is a
bounded repetition with decay, **not prediction**: past the stated maximum the output is silence, never a longer extrapolation.
A real block resets the run. Stated maximum: 8 blocks (42.7 ms) for music, 3 (16 ms) for realtime. These are provisional
engineering parameters; **no listening test exists**, so nothing here says concealment sounds acceptable.

## 6. Order, duplicates, stale epochs

Playout follows the block number, never arrival order: reordered arrivals play in frame order, a duplicate never plays twice, a
late copy of a concealed block is `late` and never played (`N04-REORDER-DUP`). Consistency with N01: a stale epoch is ignored
without touching the stream (`N04-EPOCH` shows the stream's queue, cursor and epoch unchanged around stale and future packets
and around a refused restart), a duplicate does not advance the cursor, and a packet beyond the admissible window is discarded
and counted.

## 7. Clock

`ClockView.usable == false` (N03 Unlocked or Expired): no admission, and `decide` returns `ClockUnusable` without consuming
the block (`N04-CLOCK`). A view earlier than the last accepted one is refused as `clock_regression`; after an N03 reset
the caller rebinds with `begin_stream`. This is the engine's own defence; the muted-recovery rules stay N03's.

## 8. Coordinated latency changes

Source latency changes need group coordination, so media cannot change the timeline: a header off the timeline by more than the
tolerance is `timeline_mismatch`. A change arrives only through `apply_change(LatencyChange { epoch, change_id, effective_frame,
new_anchor_ns })` from the control channel (N02/G01), and is checked in this order: bound; epoch (`stale_epoch`, `future_epoch`);
`change_id` greater than the last (`replayed_change`); no change pending (`change_pending`); `effective_frame` a multiple of 256
(`misaligned`); at least `notice_blocks` ahead of the cursor (`too_soon`) and at most `horizon_blocks` ahead (`too_far_ahead`);
anchor well-formed (`bad_anchor`); not a zero shift (`no_change`); `|shift| <= max_step_blocks` (`transition_too_large`);
`|net shift since the stream began| <= max_net_blocks` (`latency_cap_exceeded`).

* **Increase** (`shift > 0`): from `effective_frame` on, blocks are presented later; the first block after it reports
  `gap_before_ns = shift`, the silence the consumer must insert (`N04-LATENCY-UP`).
* **Decrease** (`shift < 0`): the smallest `n >= 1` blocks from `effective_frame` are `skipped` so that the next block is not
  presented before the previous one ends; it reports the remaining `gap_before_ns`. An exact fit (a shift of precisely three
  blocks' worth of time) skips three and leaves no gap (`N04-LATENCY-DOWN`, case `W13B`): the sample clock never rewinds.
* **No unbounded growth.** The queue is fixed at `horizon_blocks`; the net shift is capped at `max_net_blocks` in either
  direction (`N04-LATENCY-CAP`: two `+4` steps then a third is refused; `+1` fits and the next `+1` does not).
* **Advice, never applied.** `latency_advice` reads the last 64 arrival margins (`cutoff - arrival`): at least two late arrivals
  suggest an increase of enough whole blocks to have made the worst one on time (at most `max_step_blocks`); if that would pass
  the net cap the answer is `unhealthy`, not more latency, so one member that cannot keep up does not delay every room
  indefinitely. A full window with nothing late and spare slack beyond three blocks suggests a decrease. The engine only
  advises; applying a change needs the coordination above (`N04-ADVICE`).

## 9. Downstream and backpressure

The D01 ring is all-or-nothing and reports `ring_full`. After a decision, the caller tries to hand the block downstream;
`settle_delivery(submit_deadline, accepted_at)` records `delivered` if the ring took it no later than the deadline, otherwise
`abandoned`. The block is never retried past its deadline and nothing is queued for it, so backpressure cannot grow memory,
and abandoning a delivery does not undo or repeat a decision (`N04-BACKPRESSURE`). All vectors use positive `work_ns` and `lead_ns`.

## 14. Not covered, and why

1. **The legacy `BoundedJitterBuffer`** (`lib.rs`, N01's baseline) is untouched and still serves `tests/n01_loopback.rs`. The
   `franken-speaker` virtual-house harness no longer uses it: `playout_adapter.rs` puts the real `PlayoutEngine` in its place
   (Q01 `ffw-gmq`; written, awaiting its first central run). The buffer does not meet this contract: it takes `consume_by` from the packet
   (`push_packet`), stores `mode` and never reads it, accepts any `max_blocks`, evicts the oldest block to admit a newer one,
   admits a recovered block without a deadline check, and conceals by repeating the previous block with `0.7^n` for up to eight
   blocks in `f32`. Retiring it is outside this slice.
   **How the harness uses the engine, and what that is not.** Each member's sender runs about 93 blocks ahead of its render
   loop, because the engine decides a block `work + lead` before its presentation, about 496 ms after it was sent; the seeded
   network schedule, the N01 sender and `StreamReceiver::accept` are still called once per block, in block order. Packets reach
   the engine in arrival order (so jitter reorders them and the schedule's duplicates arrive twice at N01, whose replay window refuses the second copy), each with a `ClockView`
   built from that node's own `GroupClock` at its own simulated local time; a reading that falls is raised and counted
   (`clamped_readings`). A block played as real must carry the bytes the sender sent (digest taken at send time), and every
   block position must be decided exactly once. The per-block counts are checked against the schedule by rules restated in
   the test, not read from the engine. This is synthetic host integration: the render costs (1.5 ms work, 2 ms lead) are
   illustrative, nothing is shown about a device, a provider or audio output, and concealment quality is not evaluated.
   **Arrival-time authentication and the source commit.** The harness stages each datagram RAW and authenticates it when it arrives, with the N01 receiver of its stream generation: the node's own receiver for the latest generation, and for an earlier one the COPY of the receiver that the commit took before it rebound the node's receiver to the new stream (N01 holds one stream at a time), kept while that generation's below-cutover tail is owed and dropped when it retires. A datagram is routed by the hint `franken_stream::untrusted_route_hint` reads from its own header (attacker-controlled, allocation-free, never an admission, never a replay-window update); the selected receiver still verifies MAC, identity, epoch and replay. Old-stream datagrams still in flight at the commit are therefore played (`accepted_by_draining_generation`), a duplicate's second copy is N01's replay refusal (`replay_refused_at_receiver`, never an engine duplicate), any other receiver refusal is `refused_at_receiver`, an authenticated block outside its generation's interval (the replaced adapter's blocks at or after the cutover) is `outside_generation_refused` (the datagram is authenticated on a trial copy of the generation's receiver, kept only when N01 refused it or the block is permitted, so such a datagram cannot push the replay window past the tail the generation still owes, and it is not in the receiver's own counters), and a retired generation's datagrams are refused by name. The copy is charged to the member's ledger line beside the engines (`size_of::<StreamReceiver>()` per retained copy; a logical count of retained receiver values in the host simulation, not process RAM: the `Option<StreamReceiver>` slot is inline in every generation entry, so a dropped copy releases its charge and shrinks no allocation, and each datagram's trial copy is one bounded temporary stack value that is not charged), and a copy's counters are not summed with the node receiver's. **Group epoch.** Every generation and its queued blocks are bound to the trusted group epoch (`GroupClock::epoch`, read from the clock the engines are driven by, never from a datagram) captured at initial binding or candidate preparation. The binding is mandatory even for the initial/latest generation without a retained receiver. Commit preserves the prepared candidate's binding and never refreshes a replaced generation's epoch. When it moves, the generation is fenced: its copy is dropped, its engine is re-bound to the stream epoch it already had at its own cursor (which clears its queue, so the blocks it still owes are decided with no history and never played), and datagrams routed to it are refused by name (`group_epoch_fenced_refused`; `group_epoch_fenced_generations` counts the fences). All stale nonretired generations are fenced, including the newest; same-epoch source changes still drain normally. An already fenced generation cannot reacquire a retained receiver. A newly prepared authorized stream in the new group epoch can resume valid output; candidate preparation before an epoch change cannot be relabeled by a later commit. The epoch-fence tests exercise queued audio with no new arrivals, delayed raw old traffic, and a fresh-epoch permitted restart. These are equal-timeline host-clock tests, not evidence of automatic retiming across arbitrary clock-origin changes. Not changed: the planted late packet is still authenticated when it is sent (by a scratch copy, so the real replay window is not pushed past the arrivals it has yet to see), the planted stale-epoch and stale-stream faults still act on the node's receiver at send time, and no production code routes datagrams to per-generation `MediaPlayout` instances yet; this describes the simulation's composition, not a deployed router.

## 15. Diagnostics (`ffw.log.v1`)

**Finite event-rate admission (`n04.log-rate.v1`).** Every logger has independent ordinary and critical token buckets, each
with 1024 events/second and a maximum initial/refilled burst of 2048 events. These explicit provisional numbers are not measured
board capacity. Nominal 48 kHz / 256-frame playback is 187.5 decisions/second; a real decision plus delivery is 375 ordinary
events/second, leaving declared headroom for refusals. A class can admit no more than its burst plus its rate times elapsed
time in any interval. Ordinary traffic cannot spend critical credit, preserving critical reservation even during an ordinary
flood; critical traffic is finite too, and losing any critical event makes evidence incomplete.

Admission is O(1), before formatting, line-size checks, ring copying or any sink call. Oversize/full-ring attempts spend credit
too, so they cannot evade the formatting-work bound. Two fixed buckets add 48 inline bytes (already included by the engine's
`size_of` ledger), no allocations, sleeps or clock reads. Caller-provided group time is clamped monotonically; backwards/zero
time mints no credit, and a large elapsed time fills only to the burst. Integer fractional credit is exact. A full-refill branch
avoids multiplication overflow even at `u64::MAX`. The existing quarter-ring critical reserve remains unchanged.

`dropped_rate` counts every rate refusal and is included in `dropped()` and the human summary. `emitted = delivered + queued +
dropped_full + dropped_oversize + dropped_rate` remains exact. Rate-refused critical events also increment `critical_lost`;
there is no critical-loss waiver. For observation compatibility the new serialized counter is omitted only when zero; existing
healthy vector decisions, JSON line bytes and digests are unchanged. `N04-LOG-LOSS` requires the actual native rate test and
its `n04-log-rate` record rederived from independent integer-credit Python; controls cover zero time, fractional refill,
backwards/maximum time, reserved critical admission, pre-format refusal, full-ring loss and critical-rate loss. `N04-NO-ALLOC`
also requires the allocator probe of both permitted and rate-refused paths. Python judge controls are not native execution.

Code: `playout/log.rs`; model: `LogWriter` and `LogRing` in `tests/n04/reference.py`. Off by default; `enable_log(LogConfig)` turns it
on and is the log's only allocation (`capacity` lines of up to 1024 bytes).

**What is recorded.** One line per decision and per refusal, each written by production code at the call that made it:

| `event_kind` | when | critical | `cause` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `n04.decision.<outcome>` | `decide` returned a decision (`real`, `concealed`, `muted_gap`, `muted_nohistory`, `muted_overdue`, `skipped`) | all but `real` | `no_block_by_cutoff`, `gap_longer_than_conceal_maximum`, `nothing_played_yet`, `decided_at_or_after_presentation`, `latency_decrease` |
| `n04.poll.<clock_unusable\|clock_regression\|not_bound>` | `decide` could not decide | yes | none |
| `n04.refusal.<admission code>` | `on_packet` or `on_repair` did not admit (never for `admitted`) | no | none |
| `n04.stream.started` / `n04.stream.refused` | `begin_stream` | yes | refusal code |
| `n04.change.accepted` / `n04.change.refused` | `apply_change` | yes | refusal code |
| `n04.delivery.<delivered\|abandoned>` | `settle_delivery` | `abandoned` only | none |

`Pending` and an admitted packet are neither a decision nor a refusal and write nothing. `advice()` and `snapshot()` write nothing.

**The line** is the shared `ffw.log.v1` event (`franken_test_support::DiagnosticEvent`), fields in its order: `schema_version`,
`timestamp_mono_ns`, `run_id`, `case_id`, `correlation_id` (`<member pseudonym>#<block>`), `member_id`, `group_id`, `source_epoch` (the
bound epoch, 0 if none), `state_revision` (blocks decided so far), `event_kind`, `message`, `queue_depth` (occupied slots after the
call), `latency_us` (how late a decision was taken, a late arrival's lateness, or an abandoned delivery's overshoot; else 0), then
`fault` and `cause` only when present. The timestamp is the group time the caller passed, never earlier than the previous record's
(the start, change and delivery-without-time records reuse the previous one). The `message` is built from fixed words, `key=value`
pairs and numbers only: **nothing a peer supplied is copied** (payload bytes, strings), and the identifiers are the caller's
pseudonyms (`ffw.pseudonym.v1`: `p-` and 16 hex characters of the keyed SHA-256). `LogConfig` refuses anything else as a member or group,
so a raw identifier cannot reach a line, and the key never enters the engine. Labels are `[a-z0-9][a-z0-9._-]{0,63}`.

**The sink never blocks `decide`.** The decision path formats the line into a preallocated ring and returns; it never calls a sink,
because `drain_log(sink, budget)` is the only way to reach one and it is called from outside the render loop, with at most
`budget` offers per call. A sink replies `Taken`, `Full` or `Unavailable`; the last two leave the line queued and end that drain.
So a slow, full, unavailable or blocking sink stalls only whoever drains, and the decisions are the ones taken with no log.
This is a structural guarantee plus tests, not a measurement: no real sink was timed.

**Loss is counted.** Every record is delivered, queued or dropped, and `emitted == delivered + queued + dropped_full +
dropped_oversize` (`LogStats::accounted`). Ordinary records may use all but a quarter of the ring (`capacity / 4` slots are kept for critical
ones); critical records may use all of it. A line longer than the bound (default 1024, configurable lower) is dropped whole, never
cut. `critical_lost` counts critical records among the dropped; when it is not zero, `evidence_complete()` is false and `Display`
says `INCOMPLETE`: the run's log can no longer be trusted as complete.

**Tests.** The same 24 worlds run with the log on and a sink drained after every op (`keeps_up`: the paired positive), and 12 sink
scenarios (`log_scenarios` in the vectors): seven with hand-derived counts (a ring of 4, 2 and 1 with no drain, a ring of 4 whose
kept room admits a critical record after an ordinary one was refused, a sink that is always unavailable, always full, and one that
keeps up), and five held to the reference alone (a sink that recovers, an oversize bound, a slow drain, a flapping sink, a full sink
in the realtime policy). Each scenario also requires the decisions to equal the no-log run's, and the delivered and still-queued
lines to be the full log with whole lines removed, in order. `n04_log_accounting_holds_after_every_op` re-checks the identity after
every op of every run, so a drop that went uncounted shows at the op that made it. `n04_log_a_blocking_sink_is_never_called_on_the_decision_path`
runs a 700-block world through a full, unserved ring on a watchdog thread (a producer that waited would hang it), then offers a
sink that costs a second of virtual time per offer a drain budget of zero after every op and of three at the end: the cost is
exactly three seconds, all of it in the drain. The golden lines in `playout/log/tests.rs` (five) and `tests/n04/anchors.py` (four scripts, 22 lines: every line shape
except `concealed`/`muted_gap`/`muted_overdue` decisions, `delivered`, and the `clock_regression`/`not_bound` polls) were assembled by hand, and the pseudonyms recomputed with `hashlib`.

**Planted negatives, and what catches them.** On the reference (the model the Rust is held to), 30 single plants were each applied to a
scratch copy: a drop that is not counted, a ring that never drops (the model of a producer that waits for room), a drain that keeps
offering after `Full` or loses the line when the sink is `Unavailable`, a budget off by one, no kept room for critical records, a
lost critical record not counted, an exclusive line bound, timestamps that go backwards, a pseudonym that does not hash the identifier
length, wrong criticality, and a dozen wrong fields in the lines (lateness, queue, revision, cause, correlation, sign of a delta). The
first run caught 23; the seven survivors showed missing hand-derived expectations (the reserve, a taken line leaving the ring, a
late decision's `latency_us`, a poll's block, a negative delta, a repair's source, a skipped block's cause), which became two scenarios
and scripts C and D. All 30 are now rejected. This says the expectations are sharp; it says nothing about the Rust, which cannot be
mutated on the host (no local builds). The Rust's own negatives are the tests above, and the judge refuses a fabricated log that shows
an unaccounted drop, a decision-path offer or virtual time, a decision changed by the sink, a missing line or a line that differs from the
reference's (`tests/n04/test_judge.py`, which is also synthetic).

**Not shown.** That a real writer keeps up; the time formatting takes on the target ISA; that the ring's memory fits a board's budget
(1 KiB per line is a choice of the caller's `capacity`); any rate limit. See section 14, item 5.
