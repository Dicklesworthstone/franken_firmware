# Contract G01 — Unified Group Membership State Machine (`ffw-kh0`)

**Status: two slices implemented offline in `crates/franken-session/src/membership.rs`, both committed.
Slice 1 is `a23115f` (central `cargo test -p franken-session` 26/0, clippy clean). Slice 2 is `a8cb610`
(central run b17, as reported by the orchestrator: `franken-session` 43/0, workspace 785 passed, clippy
and fmt clean). The bead is open and blocked in the tracker.** Slice 1 is the section 12
core: the membership cutover protocol, the authority check on every mutation, the
missing-acknowledgement policy, exclusion as an unresolved output grant, epoch advance, and
stable-intent persistence. Slice 2 is the section 12 lease clause: quorum-acknowledged lease renewal,
takeover, partitioned leaders, stale-coordinator fencing, and the lease-expiry playback policy. A third
slice, source ownership arbitration (section 10), is written but has never been compiled. The
rest of the bead (section 9) is not done. Nothing here touches a device, a network, or a provider,
and no claim is made about which boards or firmware support any of it.

The module is additive. `UnifiedGroupSession::apply_command`, `SessionError` and
`GroupCommandPayload` are unchanged; the engine has its own `MembershipError`. The N02 control
channel and this engine therefore never share a sequence space. A default `MembershipConfig` behaves
exactly as slice 1 did: one configured issuer, no electorate, `clock_uncertainty_ns = 0`.

## 1. Authority

A group has one **configured issuer** per incarnation: the session's `coordinator_id`. Every mutation
presents an `AuthorityToken { group_id, issuer_id, epoch, expires_at_ns }`. `AuthorityToken::check`
(= `check_with_margin(.., 0)`) is pure and refuses, in this order:

| Condition | Error |
|---|---|
| `group_id` differs | `WRONG_GROUP` |
| `epoch` below the group's | `STALE_AUTHORITY` |
| `epoch` above the group's | `FUTURE_AUTHORITY` (a follower never mints a valid epoch) |
| `issuer_id` is not the configured issuer | `NOT_CONFIGURED_ISSUER` |
| `now_ns + margin_ns >= expires_at_ns` | `AUTHORITY_EXPIRED` |

Slice 2 moved the epoch checks ahead of the issuer check. A coordinator that has been replaced names
the old issuer *and* the old epoch; refusing it as `NOT_CONFIGURED_ISSUER` would hide the real
reason, which is that it was fenced by epoch. Every engine mutation goes through one `authorize`
rule that passes `margin_ns = lease.clock_uncertainty_ns`, so a holder stops acting `u` before its
lease ends (section 6).

A refused call changes nothing. Token *authenticity* is the transport's job (N01/N02); this layer
decides whether the token is *current*. A group mints new epochs by exactly one rule, chosen by
`MembershipConfig::epoch_mint` and never mixed:

- `EpochMint::ConfiguredIssuer` (default): `advance_epoch` is the only way to a newer epoch. It needs
  the issuer's current token, aborts any proposal in flight (`EPOCH_ADVANCED`), resets the session's
  command record (the record belongs to the old epoch) and bumps `state_revision` once. The quorum
  entry points of section 6 refuse (`NOT_A_QUORUM_GROUP`).
- `EpochMint::Quorum`: only a voter-quorum win mints an epoch (`assume_authority`), or a node learns
  of one (`adopt_authority`). `advance_epoch` refuses (`QUORUM_REQUIRED`): an issuer bumping the epoch
  alone while a quorum elects someone else at the same epoch would make two leaders.

## 2. The cutover protocol

One change (join or leave) is in flight at a time (`PROPOSAL_PENDING`).

1. **Propose** `(change, cutover_frame, ack_deadline_ns)`. Checked before anything is recorded: the
   member is absent (join) or present (leave), a joiner's board is eligible, the group is not full
   (`MAX_GROUP_MEMBERS` = 32, provisional), a joiner has no unresolved earlier output, the cutover is
   at least `min_cutover_lead_frames` (4,800, provisional) ahead, and the deadline is in the future.
   **Nothing audible changes.**
2. **Ack** from each participant (every current member, plus the joiner): `ReadinessReport` with
   `format_ok`, `buffer_ok`, `clock_confident`, `can_stop_flush` and the frame at which the member's
   irrevocable already-submitted output ends. A repeat of the same report is idempotent; a different
   one is `ACK_CONFLICT`; a stranger is `NOT_A_PARTICIPANT`; no new ack is accepted after the deadline.
3. **Commit** needs every non-excluded participant acknowledged (`MISSING_ACKS`) and ready
   (`MEMBER_NOT_READY`), the cutover still ahead (`CUTOVER_PASSED`), and the cutover not before any
   member's output tail (`CUTOVER_BEFORE_OUTPUT_TAIL`). **Still nothing audible changes.**
4. **Activate** at the first `tick` with `frame >= cutover_frame`: the change is applied to the
   session in one step and `state_revision` increases by exactly 1. A joiner enters
   `SynchronizedReady` (it validated clock confidence in its report). If the group changed so that the
   committed change no longer applies, it aborts with `PRECONDITION_FAILED`.

Members, their lifecycle states and `state_revision` are byte-identical through steps 1 to 3. A
proposal can be aborted by the authority at any time before activation (`BY_AUTHORITY`), which leaves
the session exactly as it was: a cancelled join never disturbs playing members. An uncommitted
proposal whose cutover frame arrives lapses (`CUTOVER_MISSED`), so an authority that never commits
cannot hold the group. Settled results stay queryable by proposal id for the last 64 proposals
(`MAX_TERMINAL_HISTORY`); older ids answer `UNKNOWN_PROPOSAL`, explicitly.

### 2.1 Refusals of the cutover protocol

Every refusal carries a stable code (`MembershipError::code`) and only identifiers and numbers. Besides
the authority codes of section 1, the cutover protocol refuses with:

| Code | Refused when |
|---|---|
| `PROPOSAL_PENDING` | a change is already in flight |
| `UNKNOWN_PROPOSAL`, `NOT_PENDING` | the id is not remembered (older than the last 64), or the proposal is no longer pending (the refusal carries its phase) |
| `MEMBER_ALREADY_PRESENT` | a join names a member already in the group |
| `UNKNOWN_MEMBER` | a leave, or an output grant, names a member not in the group |
| `INELIGIBLE_BOARD` | the joiner's board fails the offline admission gate |
| `GROUP_FULL` | the group is already at its maximum |
| `OUTPUT_UNRESOLVED` | the joiner was excluded earlier and its output is not yet resolved |
| `CUTOVER_TOO_SOON` | the cutover is earlier than now plus `min_cutover_lead_frames` |
| `ACK_DEADLINE_NOT_IN_FUTURE` | the acknowledgement deadline is not after now |
| `NOT_A_PARTICIPANT`, `ACK_CONFLICT`, `ACK_AFTER_DEADLINE` | an acknowledgement from a stranger or an excluded member, a different repeat of one, or a new one at or after the deadline |
| `MISSING_ACKS`, `MEMBER_NOT_READY`, `CUTOVER_PASSED`, `CUTOVER_BEFORE_OUTPUT_TAIL` | the commit conditions of step 3 |
| `EPOCH_NOT_ADVANCING` | `advance_epoch` asks for an epoch that is not above the current one |
| `NO_UNRESOLVED_OUTPUT` | `resolve_output` names a member with no unresolved grant |

## 3. Missing acknowledgements, exclusion, and unresolved output

At the ack deadline `tick` applies the configured `MissingAckPolicy`:

- `Abort` (default): the proposal aborts, the group is unchanged.
- `ExcludeUnreachable`: existing members that did not acknowledge are excluded; the proposal
  continues without them and removes them at the cutover.
- An unreachable **joiner** aborts under either policy.

An excluded member is **not** evidence of silence. Each one is recorded as an unresolved output grant
with an unknown bound. `conflicting_start_gate(now_frame)` refuses a conflicting program while any
such grant is unknown or its bound lies ahead; `resolve_output` records a stop acknowledgement or a
conservative expiry bound (the caller supplies output expiry plus irrevocable tail, clock uncertainty
and stop/drain lead). Evidence can never shorten a bound already required, and an excluded member
cannot be re-admitted while its output is unresolved (`OUTPUT_UNRESOLVED`).

## 4. Persistence and restart

Persisted **membership** state is only `MembershipIntent { group_id, issuer_id, members: [{ member_id,
profile_id, role }] }`. It has no lease, queue, acknowledgement, lifecycle or volume. After a restart,
`GroupMembership::after_restart` refuses every mutation (`AUTHORITY_NOT_DISCOVERED`) until
`discover_authority` is given a token the live group accepts, or, in a quorum group, until
`adopt_authority` learns who holds the current epoch. A restored intent lists members to re-admit
(`members_missing_from`); it is not a restored live session. How and where the intent is stored is
U01's decision.

**One deliberate exception: the voter record.** A lease voter's grant is a fencing promise, not a
convenience, and forgetting it can let the same epoch be granted twice. `DurableVoterRecord`
persists exactly three things: the voter's id, the lease it last confirmed (epoch, holder, expiry),
and the takeover it last granted (epoch, candidate, expiry, and the previous term's expiry it
observed). It must reach durable storage **before** the acknowledgement that depends on it is sent.
`LeaseVoter::after_restart` refuses to answer anything (`VOTER_RECORD_UNAVAILABLE`) until `restore`
is given that voter's own record; another voter's record does not unlock it. A group that cannot keep
this record cannot keep voters, and so cannot run `EpochMint::Quorum`. Where and how it is stored (and
the write ordering) is U01's decision; this layer only states the requirement and fails closed.

## 5. Interaction with command traffic

Membership activation, epoch advance, `assume_authority` and a forward `adopt_authority` bump
`state_revision` like an applied command, so a client holding an older expected revision is refused
by the N02 channel's revision check. That is intended. None of them consumes a `command_seq`.

## 6. Leases, quorum renewal, takeover and partitions (slice 2)

### 6.1 Fault model and what is assumed

- **Crash and partition faults only.** Nodes stop, restart, and lose messages; messages that arrive
  are authentic (the shared group key and N01/N02 provide that). A Byzantine group member is out of
  scope; the contract makes no claim about one.
- **Clocks.** Each node reads its own monotonic clock. `LeaseTerms::clock_uncertainty_ns` (`u`) is a
  *declared* bound on how far apart any two nodes' readings of one instant may be. **Safety holds only
  while the real pairwise skew is at most `u`.** The default `u = 0` assumes perfectly agreeing clocks,
  which no deployment has; it keeps behaviour exact until N03 supplies a measured bound. Nothing here
  measures a clock.
- **The electorate** is a configured `QuorumConfig` (group id and voter ids). A quorum is a strict
  majority. An even electorate can split with nobody winning; that is a refusal (`NO_QUORUM`), never a
  tie. Use an odd number. Changing the electorate within an incarnation is not modelled, and which
  nodes are voters is configuration, not derived from the session's members.

### 6.2 The rules

| # | Rule | Where |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | A holder extends its lease only with acknowledgements from a quorum (`QuorumRound`). Alone it cannot. | `renew_request`, `QuorumRound`, `renew_lease` |
| R2 | A voter acknowledges a renewal only of a lease it knows to be the requester's (the confirmed lease, or the takeover it granted that requester), never of an epoch below the highest it has seen or granted, and never of an epoch it has not heard of. Acknowledging extends its view of the lease. | `acknowledge_renewal` |
| R3 | A voter grants a takeover only for the next epoch (`highest seen or granted + 1`), only to one candidate per epoch, and only once its own clock reads at least the latest lease expiry it knows plus `u`. A repeat from the same candidate is answered the same. | `grant_takeover` |
| R4 | A holder treats its lease as over `u` before its expiry (`now + u >= expires_at_ns`). | `authorize` |
| R5 | A round is over once the lease it proposed could no longer be relied on (`now >= proposed_expiry - u`); a late quorum is no quorum. | `QuorumRound::check_live` |
| R6 | Tokens are judged by epoch first. A node learns a newer epoch with `adopt_authority`, forward only; a second holder for one epoch is `CONFLICTING_LEADERS`. | `check_with_margin`, `adopt_authority` |
| R7 | The winner of a takeover applies it with `assume_authority`: the epoch must exceed the session's, the lease must still have `u` to run, any proposal in flight aborts (`EPOCH_ADVANCED`), and conflicting programs stay gated (section 6.4). The engine does not announce; the caller has the winner's own voter and every voter it can reach `observe` the new lease (otherwise the winner's voter keeps only its grant and can neither renew nor retake). | `assume_authority`, `LeaseVoter::observe` |
| R8 | Epochs are minted by one rule per group (section 1). | `EpochMint` |
| R9 | A voter's record is durable and fails closed (section 4). | `DurableVoterRecord` |

### 6.3 Why two leaders cannot overlap

Any renewal quorum and any takeover quorum are majorities of one electorate, so they share a voter
`V` (R1, R3). `V` orders them. If `V` acknowledged the renewal first (R2), it holds the longer expiry
`E` and refuses a takeover until its clock reads `E + u` (R3). If `V` granted the takeover first, it
refuses the renewal of the older epoch (R2), so the holder never obtains the quorum. When `V` finally
grants, its clock reads at least `E + u`; with skew at most `u`, the old holder's clock reads at least
`E`, and it stopped acting at `E - u` (R4). The new leader's lease starts only after the grants.
Two candidates for one epoch cannot both win: each voter grants one candidate per epoch (R3) and two
majorities share a voter. The unit and seeded tests below check this at the boundary instants, with
hand-computed numbers and a planted failure.

The price is a gap with **no leader** between a holder's last live instant and the earliest instant a
taker can be granted: `[E - u, E + u)`, a width of `2u`. That is deliberate.

### 6.4 Lease expiry and playback policy

A leader's authority to start output is its lease; output already started is a separate thing, and
the policy keeps them apart.

- **No live lease, no new output.** `grant_output(member, through_frame)` needs a live lease
  (`AUTHORITY_EXPIRED` otherwise), reaches at most `max_output_horizon_frames` ahead
  (`OUTPUT_HORIZON_EXCEEDED`; 480,000 = 10 s at 48 kHz by default, provisional), and only ever extends
  an earlier grant.
- **Granted output plays out and then mutes.** `output_permitted(member, frame)` is true only below a
  frame the member was granted. When a lease lapses the member plays exactly what it was already
  granted and then mutes. Nothing new is issued.
- **A new leader waits out the old one's output.** `assume_authority` records
  `previous term's latest expiry + max_output_horizon_ns + u` (default 10 s, provisional) as the
  earliest instant a *conflicting* program may start (`takeover_start_gate`). The frame horizon and the
  nanosecond horizon describe one duration and must agree; the defaults do (tested), and a deployment
  that changes one changes the other.

`output_permitted` and `takeover_start_gate` are pure queries. Nothing consumes them yet (section 9).

### 6.5 What each rule is for, honestly

The two-leader invariant above rests on R1, R3's expiry wait, R2's view extension, R3's one candidate
per epoch, R4's expiry in the holder's own clock, and R9. Three rules are **not** needed for it when
`u` bounds the skew, and are kept as defence in depth or hygiene, each held by a direct unit test:

- **R4's holder margin.** The taker's wait alone orders the terms when skew is at most `u`; the holder
  margin extends the tolerated skew to `2u` in the worst case (`without_the_uncertainty_margin_...`
  shows the window `[1000 + u, 1040 - u)` for a 40 ns skew). The contract does not promise that slack.
- **R2's refusal of older-epoch renewals.** A voter that granted epoch `e+1` has, by R3, already seen
  the old term lapse, and the old holder's own engine will not apply a renewal after its lease ends.
  The refusal stops a stale holder from gathering a renewal quorum out of voters that have already
  granted its successor, and it fails closed if the skew bound is ever exceeded.
- **R3's next-epoch-only.** Skipping epochs would still be fenced by epoch; the rule bounds epoch growth
  and refuses a malformed request. It is not a safety rule.

### 6.6 Liveness is not promised

A leader appears only when a majority is reachable, a lease has lapsed, and one candidate asks. Split
votes, simultaneous candidates and partitions without a majority all end in refusals with no leader;
there is no randomized backoff or election timer here, so two candidates can keep splitting a vote.
Nothing in this layer guarantees progress.

### 6.7 Errors added by slice 2

`EMPTY_ELECTORATE`, `NOT_A_VOTER`, `VOTER_RECORD_UNAVAILABLE`, `NO_LEASE_VIEW`, `NOT_NEXT_EPOCH`,
`LEASE_NOT_EXPIRED`, `NOT_LEASE_HOLDER`, `ATTEMPT_MISMATCH`, `ATTEMPT_EXPIRED`, `NO_QUORUM`,
`LEASE_TOO_LONG`, `LEASE_NOT_EXTENDED`, `CONFLICTING_LEADERS`, `WIN_NOT_APPLICABLE`,
`OUTPUT_HORIZON_EXCEEDED`, `QUORUM_REQUIRED`, `NOT_A_QUORUM_GROUP`. A result of a quorum round
(`LeaseWin`) applies only to the token and session it was gathered for (`WIN_NOT_APPLICABLE`).

**The seeded partition schedule.** 8 seeds x 2 clock assignments (random offsets of up to 50 ns, and
the worst case: the holder 50 ns slow, everyone else 50 ns fast) x 400 steps, on 3 or 5 nodes, each
with its own voter, engine, session, token and clock. Each step advances time and does one of: renew
(usually the newest holder, sometimes a stale one), take over, attempt a mutation, or re-partition
and re-announce. After the prologue, a third of announcements are lost. After every step it counts the
nodes that consider their own lease live; two or more is a failure. It also fails if two nodes win
one epoch, if two holders are announced for one epoch, if a mutation succeeds on a lease the node
should consider over, or if a refused one is refused for any reason but stale epoch or expiry.
Random choice almost never reaches the interesting paths, so every run opens with a **prologue**
that takes them on purpose: a renewal with everyone connected; the holder cut off, getting one
acknowledgement of the quorum it needs; a takeover at the *boundary probe* (the earliest true time at
which every other node's clock has reached the holder's expiry plus `u`); the heal; the old holder's
fenced mutation; and a **duel** in which a first candidate wins epoch 3 on part of the group with its
announcement lost, and an unaware rival then asks a voter that holds only its grant for the same
epoch. Floors (about a third of the 16 runs' totals): renewals >= 70, takeovers >= 45, refused
takeovers >= 700, refused minority renewals >= 300, fenced mutations >= 230, accepted mutations >= 25,
steps with a leader >= 550 and without one >= 550, plus one of each path in every run.

**Control.** With `u = 0` and the holder 100 ns behind, the boundary probe finds two live leaders in
every seed; with `u = 100` the same schedules find none. With agreeing clocks and `u = 0` there are
none either, which is why the default configuration is exact.

**What was and was not checked.** Everything in this section is a host unit test on a virtual
timeline. The central run of `a8cb610` passed `franken-session` 43/0 (as reported by the orchestrator),
which includes the floors above; those floors were chosen from the reference model's predicted counts.
The independent reference model `tests/g01/reference.py` replays 40 hand-written scenarios
(`crates/franken-session/tests/vectors/g01_scenarios.json`) and predicts the exact counts of 32
recorded schedule runs and the scenarios each of 4 planted faults must break; `scripts/e2e/g01` runs
it. On the model it shows that every rule of the contract can be switched off and the scenarios notice
it, that the schedule notices the removal of six rules and not of the three in section 6.5 (pinned in
`tests/g01/cases.json`), and that the Rust's view-plus-grant voter and the model's ledger voter reach
identical counts on every recorded run. The model and the Rust share an author, so it checks
arithmetic and agreement with the contract text, not the design.

**The Rust replay.** `crates/franken-session/tests/g01_replay.rs` is an integration test that
interprets the same op language against the real engine through its public API. It replays all 40
scenarios, asserts every expected value and error code, reproduces the 32 recorded schedule counts
with its own driver of the same seeded schedule (the in-crate harness is private to `membership.rs`
and cannot be called from an integration test), and plants the 4 faults in its driver, not in
production code, requiring each to break at least one scenario and exactly the ones the model
predicts. That is a comparison of sets: the engine collects the broken scenarios in the vector file's
order and the model lists them sorted, so order is not compared (`same_scenarios`; the first run that
reached this assertion, b22h, failed `flipped_mint` on order alone, with identical sets), but a scenario
named twice, one missing and one swapped are each refused, and the judge applies the same rule to the
log. It also corrupts one expected number and one expected code and requires both to be
reported, and it judges refusal details strictly: a refusal with no fields (`WRONG_GROUP`) carries an
empty object, as the model's does, and the comparison forgives neither an engine that drops the numbers
the model has nor `null` where the model has an empty object (`judge_refusal`). The `corrupted` record
carries nine fields, all of which the judge requires to be true: the two corruptions, the three details
cases and the four set-comparison cases (reordered sets accepted; one id missing, one swapped, one named
twice each reported). The first central run (b20) failed 11 of the 40
scenarios, every one `details: wanted {}, got null`, because the replay converted such a refusal to
`null`; the model emits `{}` for all 35 refusals whose vector omits `details`, so the replay was
changed and the vectors were not. It prints one `G01-OBSERVED` record per result; `scripts/e2e/g01 --judge LOG` re-checks
those records against the vector file and the model without trusting the Rust's own assertions.
**Central results so far** (as reported by the orchestrator): b20 passed the seeded schedules exactly and

## 10. Source ownership arbitration (slice 3)

**Status: written without a compiler.** `crates/franken-session/src/source.rs` (the engine) and
`src/source/tests.rs` (29 unit tests) have no central result yet; `tests/g01/source_reference.py` is the
independent model and runs offline. The module is additive: `membership.rs`, `UnifiedGroupSession` and
`apply_command` are unchanged, and the arbiter reads the session by shared reference, so a source epoch
cannot reset members, volumes or protection state.

Several sources can ask to play on one group at once, and the group has one program. `SourceArbiter`
decides who owns it. It holds identities and epochs and nothing else: no audio, no transport, no
credential. A source is a `SourceId`, a kind (Spotify, AirPlay 2, line-in) and a label of 1 to 64 bytes
of `a-z`, `0-9`, `.`, `_`, `:` and `-`, so a pasted token or an address cannot be one and a refusal
(`INVALID_SOURCE_LABEL`) reports a bad label's length only. **Real Spotify, AirPlay and line-in transports are out of scope**,
which is why this clause of the bead stays partly covered (section 10.9).

### 10.1 Replace or reject, per kind and command class

A command from a source that **does not own the group**, presented at the current source epoch, is decided
by this table (row: the kind of the *sending* source). The owner is never judged by the table.

| Sender | start | seek | group-change | stop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spotify | replace | reject | reject | reject |
| AirPlay 2 | replace | reject | replace | reject |
| Line-in | reject | reject | reject | reject |

`replace` displaces the owner: a new source epoch is minted and the sender becomes the owner (after a
`stop`, nobody is). `reject` is refused as `SOURCE_REJECTED` and changes nothing. The values are
engineering placeholders, not owner-approved product policy. The table is a value
(`SourcePolicyTable`), so a cell is a one-line change; `the_policy_table_is_written_out_cell_by_cell`
compares every cell with a copy written by hand, and `changing_one_cell_changes_only_that_cell` pins that
the cells are independent.

### 10.2 Judging a command

`submit` checks, in this order, and a refused call changes nothing:

1. **Authority.** The group's token, exactly as the membership engine judges it (`WRONG_GROUP`,
   `STALE_AUTHORITY`, `FUTURE_AUTHORITY`, `NOT_CONFIGURED_ISSUER`, `AUTHORITY_EXPIRED`, with the lease's
   clock-uncertainty margin).
2. **The source epoch the sender presented.** Older than the current one is `SOURCE_EPOCH_STALE`; newer is
   `SOURCE_EPOCH_FUTURE` (a source never mints an epoch). This comes before ownership and policy, so the
   loser of a race is told it lost the race, and not that policy forbade it.
3. **Ownership and policy.** With no owner only a `start` is admitted (anything else is
   `NO_ACTIVE_SOURCE`). The owner's `start` begins a new stream, its `stop` ends it, its `seek` and
   `group-change` are applied. A non-owner is decided by the table.
4. **The epoch counter.** A minting command at the largest `u64` is `SOURCE_EPOCH_EXHAUSTED`.

A command **mints** (takes the next source epoch) when it begins a stream (a `start`, also by the owner),
ends one (a `stop`, so the stopped stream's late blocks are fenced) or replaces the owner. A `seek` or a
`group-change` by the owner does not. A source epoch number is never reused. An admitted command returns
`Admitted`, the receipt without which the sender's adapter may not act: the new epoch, the owner, the
source it displaced (so the caller can stop that adapter; nothing here tells it) and whether it minted.

A `group-change` admitted here is permission to propose the membership change through the cutover
protocol (section 2). It does not alter members itself.

### 10.3 A replaced source cannot publish

`check_publish` is the gate every block passes before it may reach output. In order: nothing admitted yet,
or no owner, is `NO_ACTIVE_SOURCE`; a group epoch that has advanced is `GROUP_EPOCH_ADVANCED`; a block
older than the current epoch is `SOURCE_REPLACED` when the sender is not the owner (the typed cause: it
was replaced) and `SOURCE_EPOCH_STALE` when it is (its own earlier stream); a newer one is
`SOURCE_EPOCH_FUTURE`; the right epoch from a non-owner is `NOT_SOURCE_OWNER`. Only the owner, under the
current epoch, passes. A refused block is simply not delivered: `output_receives_only_blocks_the_gate_admitted`
drives a sink that obeys the gate. Wiring the gate into the HAL and the speaker is not done (10.9).

### 10.4 The group epoch and restarts

The arbiter is bound to the group epoch of the last command it admitted. When the group's epoch has
advanced (a takeover, section 6), the old program is void: no block passes, and a command sees no owner
and one source epoch number spent on the transition, until a source starts again at that epoch. Nothing
is stored for this, so a refused command cannot bind the arbiter to the new group epoch. After a restart
`SourceArbiter::resume_after` starts idle above a durable floor that the caller supplies, so a block of a
stream from before the restart can never match a new one; the store for the floor (U01) does not exist.

### 10.5 Concurrency: one source epoch per observed epoch

Every command carries the source epoch its sender last saw, and is judged against the current one first
(10.2, step 2). Of any set of commands that raced from the same view, the first that is admissible and
mints takes the next epoch, and every later one is stale, so **at most one of them mints**. A command
rejected by policy does not use the epoch up, so the next admissible one still can. A seek and a start
that raced are ordered by arrival: the seek first is applied and then the start takes the group; the
start first makes the seek a command from the losing epoch, which is refused. The loser learns the
current epoch from the refusal and may retry deliberately.

All cases are in `source/tests.rs`; expectations are hand-written unless noted.

**The seeded interleavings.** 8 seeds of 600 steps. Four sources (a Spotify, two AirPlay 2, a line-in)
each remember only what they were last told, so their views go stale on their own; each step is a
command, a publish (sometimes a late block of the stream the source owned before), a refresh of one
source's view, a racing round of 2 to 4 commands that all saw the same epoch, a group-epoch advance, or a
command under a token that is not current. The properties are computed from the receipts and not from
the arbiter's own view: a refused call changed nothing; the code of a refusal follows from the epoch
presented; every admitted command presented the epoch that was current; a minted epoch is the next one
and is never minted twice; a takeover was allowed by the table and displaced the previous owner; a
`start` always begins a stream and a `stop` always ends it; nothing but a `start` is admitted after a
group-epoch advance; a block reaches output only from the owner under the current epoch; an older block
from a non-owner is refused as `SOURCE_REPLACED`; and **a racing round admits at most one command that
mints**. Floors are about a third of the reference's totals (the Rust test states them).

**Planted negatives, in copies of the code.** The engine cannot be mutated here without a compiler, so
the three defects are planted in `Transcription`, a second, independently written copy of the engine's
decisions inside the test module: a block admitted whenever some source owns the group
(`ReplacedSourcePublishes`), a `start` that need not present the current epoch
(`RacingStartsBothAdmitted`), and a `seek` applied whenever some source owns the group
(`LosingSeekApplied`). With no defect the copy must agree with `SourceArbiter` on every outcome of every
seeded step (`the_sound_transcription_behaves_step_for_step_like_the_engine`), which is what makes it a
copy; with each defect the schedule must report it, and the hand-written scenarios must break
(`each_planted_defect_is_caught_by_the_schedule`,
`the_planted_defects_also_break_the_hand_written_scenarios`). The same three defects are planted in the
Python model as switches, with 13 more.

### 10.9 Not done

- **Real transports.** Spotify, AirPlay 2 and line-in are identities and epochs here. Nothing opens a
  session, authenticates a source, or stops a replaced adapter.
- **Enforcement at the output.** `check_publish` exists and nothing in the HAL, the speaker or the
  receivers calls it; no receiver compares a source epoch carried on the wire.
- **Persistence.** The durable epoch floor `resume_after` takes has no store (U01).
- **Policy.** The table is a placeholder. Whether a line-in start should preempt a streamed source, and
  who may re-group, are product decisions for the owner.
- **Notification.** The loser of a race learns the current epoch from its refusal; a replaced owner is not
  told until its next command or block is refused.
- **Executing a group change.** An admitted `group-change` is not yet joined to a membership proposal.
