D01 types are reused, not duplicated: `FRAMES_PER_BLOCK`, `MAX_DRIFT_PPM`, `sanitize_sample`,

older `BoundedDriftResampler` in `franken-dsp/src/lib.rs` (linear interpolation, a per-sample fractional

retiring the old one is not part of this bead.

## 1. Units and conventions

* Ratio in **ppb**; positive means the source runs fast, so the resampler must consume input faster.
* `step` = input frames consumed per output frame = `1 + ppb/1e9`, Q32 fixed point, truncated toward zero.
* Local time in **microseconds**; source position is a wrapping `u32` frame counter (wraps near 24.86 h at 48 kHz).
* 1 ppm of ratio is 0.00173 cent of pitch; the whole default range of ±500 ppm is ±0.87 cent.

## 2. Estimator (`franken_clock::rate`)

Least-squares slope of unwrapped position against local microseconds over at most 64 observations, in
exact 128-bit integers. `ratio_ppb = round((Dxy*1e6 - N*Dx)*1e9 / (N*Dx))`, halves away from zero, where
`Dx = n*Sxx - Sx^2`, `Dxy = n*Sxy - Sx*Sy`, `N` the nominal rate. Uncertainty is the standard error of the
slope from the fit residuals (f64, ceiling, below 0.001 ppb reported as 0), and grows by `aging_ppb_per_s`
with the age of the newest observation. No estimate is offered until `min_observations` and `min_span_us`.

| Default (48 kHz) | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `max_correction_ppm` | 500 | correction clamp |
| `rejoin_ppm` | 2000 | beyond this: rebuffer or rejoin, do not resample |
| `max_instant_ppm` | 50000 | instantaneous rate outside this is a discontinuity |
| `max_gap_us` | 10 s | longest spacing between observations |
| `max_residual_frames` | nominal/100 (480) | distance from the fitted line that is a discontinuity |
| `min_observations` / `min_span_us` | 8 / 4 s | warm-up |
| `max_uncertainty_ppb` | 50000 | above this the estimate is `Noisy` and the correction is zero |
| `aging_ppb_per_s` / `max_holdover_us` | 100 / 60 s | uncertainty growth; no estimate beyond |

**Status** precedence: `RejoinRequired` (|ratio| > rejoin) > `Noisy` > `Clamped` (|ratio| > correction limit) > `Valid`.

**Discontinuities**, checked in this order: `LocalClockRegression` (local time did not advance),
`SourceTimelineAmbiguous` (the wrapping step is negative as an `i32`: a backward jump, or forward by half the
range or more), `ObservationGap`, `ImplausibleRate`, `PositionJump` (needs the warm-up window). A step of less
than half the range that crosses the wrap is **not** a discontinuity: it is unwrapped and counted
(`stats.wraps`). A rejected observation changes nothing and **parks** the estimator: further `observe` calls fail
with `EpochTransitionRequired` until `begin_epoch`. The previous estimate stays readable (holdover).

**Reset.** `begin_epoch(epoch, reason)` is the only thing that clears the window, fit and estimate. It needs
an epoch that advances in wrapping order, records `{from, to, reason, observations_dropped}` in a bounded
log (8), and keeps configuration, counters and the log. Another estimator instance is untouched.

## 3. Resampler (`franken_dsp::resample`)

* **Ratio limit.** `max_ratio_ppm` (default 500, hard maximum 10000). Targets beyond it are clamped and counted.
* **Bounded slew.** Every 32 output frames the ratio moves toward the target by at most
  `max_slew_ppm_per_block * 125` ppb, i.e. **at most 2 ppm per 256 output frames by default** (hard maximum 50).
  Because the schedule is counted in output frames, it does not depend on how calls are split. Slewing across
  the full ±500 ppm range takes 2000 steps (about 1.3 s) one way; the whole range is under 0.9 cent of pitch.
* **Kernel.** Catmull-Rom cubic, taps at -1, 0, +1, +2 (exact for linear and quadratic signals), `f32`.
* **History.** A four-frame window carried across calls. Short, zero-length and final partial blocks are
  ordinary. The first output needs 3 frames pulled; the kernel's lookahead (`DELAY_FRAMES` = 2) is the fixed
  algorithmic delay to add to the presentation mapping.
* **Pull-driven.** Input is taken from a `FrameSource` only as the next output needs it. An empty source ends
  the call early (`underrun`) with state intact; resuming with more input is bit-identical to an uninterrupted run.
* **Exact counts** (constant `S`, Q32): frames pulled after `n` outputs = `3 + floor((n-1)*S / 2^32)`; outputs
  from `N` frames = `ceil((N-2)*2^32 / S)` (zero below 3). `plan(n)` computes the same state transition in closed
  form per quantum, without audio; `d03_plan_equals_render` pins it to `render`.
* **Epochs.** `begin_epoch` clears the window and phase, fades the output in over 64 frames, and keeps the
  ratio, target, counters and slew schedule (those are endpoint properties). Epochs advance in wrapping order.
* **Sample safety.** Non-finite input becomes 0 and out-of-range input is clamped, both counted; output is
  limited to ±1 and counted. A bad frame cannot become a full-scale burst. No `unsafe`, no allocation, no panic.

## 4. Quality and cost, tied to the target

Quality is a guarantee from the kernel's frequency response, not a sample of one run: for a tone of
frequency `f`, SNR is at least `-20 log10 max_t |H(w,t) - e^{jwt}|` (worst case over fractional position),
less 1 dB, capped at 110 dB (the `f32` floor). From `tests/d03/vectors.json`:

| Tone | 440 Hz | 1 kHz | 4 kHz | 10 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| guaranteed SNR | 109.0 dB | 87.8 dB | 51.1 dB | 23.5 dB |

Cost per **stereo output frame**: 35 `f32` operations (15 weight, 2 position, 14 for two 4-tap dot products,
4 clamp compares), +2 while fading in; 256 output frames = **8,960** operations per block, plus 3 integer
operations per frame and 4 compares per input frame. This is a hand count, recorded by the implementation
(`stats.float_ops`) and checked by `d03_operation_counts_are_accounted`.

**CPU on the target ISA.** Measured in guest instructions per output frame on the soft-float big-endian MIPS32r2
device target under QEMU (section 4.2): about 2,280 at the shipped ratio (0 ppb) and about 3,040 at the +/-500 ppm
limit, that is roughly 65 to 87 instructions per hand-counted `f32` operation. **Cycles on a real core: not measured:
needs hardware.** The float ABI of the boards is not derivable from headers (E01 blocker 5), so the operation count
stays the proxy for the boards the guest does not model. Trade-off: this kernel is the cheapest that meets about 88 dB at 1 kHz; music with
significant energy above 5 kHz would need a longer kernel (a windowed-sinc design costs more than the
35 operations here) or a listening test to say it is not needed. Neither is decided by this bead.

### 4.1 Quality bands, declared before the run (`D03-QUALITY`)

**Status of this section.** Written at HEAD `5d4cbc6` on 2026-11-05, **before** `crates/franken-dsp/tests/d03_quality.rs`
existed and before any figure below was measured. Every number is derived from the kernel mathematics of section 4
(the Catmull-Rom response over fractional position, evaluated in double precision by `tests/d03/reference.py`) and
from closed forms for the two planted interpolators; none comes from a run of the Rust. **If a measured figure misses
its band it is reported as a miss and the band does not move.** Changing a band means adding a new dated row and keeping
the old one with the figure that missed it.

**What is measured.** A pure sine at -1 dBFS (amplitude A = 10^(-1/20) = 0.891251, input phase 0.25 rad) goes through
`DriftResampler` at a settled ratio. Over a window of 32 768 output frames, after skipping the first 192 (the 64-frame
fade-in plus margin), a two-parameter least-squares fit of `a cos(wn) + b sin(wn)` is made at the **expected output
frequency** `w = 2 pi f0 s / 48000` per output frame (`s` = the exact Q32 step). Two figures result, both computed from the
emitted `f32` samples alone, with a small DFT-free fit written in the test (no dependency):

* **Q (THD+N), dB, larger is better:** `10 log10( (a^2 + b^2)/2 / mean((y - fit)^2) )`. Everything that is not the
  fundamental counts against it: harmonics, images, aliases, `f32` rounding, and the amplitude and phase modulation the
  interpolation causes as the fractional position drifts through its range.
* **Gain, dB:** `20 log10( sqrt(a^2 + b^2) / A )`, the level of the carrier against the input.

The window holds at least 3 full beats of the fractional position at the slowest ratio (100 ppm drifts through one whole
sample in 10 000 frames).

**Bands** (one per tone class, not one universal figure; the section after the table says why):

<!-- d03-quality-bands -->
| tone_hz | q_floor_db | gain_lo_db | gain_hi_db |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1000 | 85.0 | -0.01 | 0.01 |
| 10000 | 22.0 | -0.60 | 0.05 |
<!-- /d03-quality-bands -->

**Rationale.**

* **Q floor.** Section 4's guarantee is the worst case over fractional position of the interpolation error, less 1 dB:
  the worst error is -88.84 dB at 1 kHz and -24.49 dB at 10 kHz (guarantees 87.84 and 23.49 dB). Q removes the fixed
  part of the gain error and averages over positions, so for any ratio it is at least the guarantee. The bands are the
  guarantees minus slack for the finite window's leakage into the fit, `f32` rounding and a plain figure: **85.0**
  (2.8 dB of slack) and **22.0** (1.5 dB of slack). A real kernel that matches section 4 passes them by construction; a
  broken weight, a wrong tap, a stale history or a bad block seam cannot.
* **Gain.** Over fractional position the kernel's magnitude has its maximum, 1.000000, at position 0, and its minimum
  at 0.999993 (-0.0001 dB) at 1 kHz and 0.940358 (-0.534 dB) at 10 kHz. The fitted carrier lies between them, so the
  bands are -0.01...+0.01 dB at 1 kHz, and -0.60...+0.05 dB at 10 kHz (the worst droop plus 0.07 dB, and 0.05 dB above
  unity for `f32` and the fit).
* **Why two bands.** The kernel's error differs by more than 60 dB between the tones. One figure would be meaningless at
  1 kHz or false at 10 kHz. The 10 kHz band is a regression floor, **not a fitness claim**: 22 dB says this kernel is not
  transparent above about 5 kHz, as section 4 already states.

**Cases.** Both tones at ratios 0, +100, -100, +500 and -500 ppm (ten steady cases); after an `epoch` reset at +500 ppm
(the ramp region against the ramped ideal, and the steady region after it); and settled after a slew from 0 to +500 ppm.
**Planted negatives**, built in the test and fed the same input at the same exact positions: a nearest-neighbour
interpolator and a linear one, at +/-100 and +/-500 ppm, both tones. **A plant must miss its band**, meaning at least
one of: Q below the floor by 3 dB or more, or the carrier gain outside its band by 0.1 dB or more. At exactly 0 ppm the
fractional position is always 0 and every interpolator is exact, so that row measures the `f32` floor and the delay
only; the test runs the plants there too and **they pass**, which is why the 0 ppm row is reported and is not a test
of interpolation. The real kernel's mean interpolation error against the ideal tone is 91.6 dB at 1 kHz and 26.6 dB at
10 kHz.

**Erratum, same day, before any Rust ran (the bands above are unchanged).** The first version of this section, and the
mail that declared it, gave the plants' expected figures as "linear 56.1 dB at 1 kHz and 16.4 dB at 10 kHz, nearest
28.5 dB and 8.6 dB" and said the plants "must fail the Q band". Those four numbers are the plants' mean error-to-signal
against the ideal tone, which is **not** the declared Q metric: Q fits the carrier first, and the fit absorbs the part of
the error that is a fixed gain and phase. Computing the declared Q from first principles (the reference, before any Rust
ran) gives linear 63.9 dB at 1 kHz and **22.3 dB at 10 kHz**, nearest 28.4-28.7 dB and 8.1-8.3 dB. So at 10 kHz the
declared Q floor of 22.0 dB does **not** reject linear interpolation (it clears the floor by 0.3 dB); the declared gain
band does: linear interpolation droops the carrier by 1.23 dB there against a band of -0.60...+0.05 dB. That is the reason
the gain is a separate band, and it is now also the reason a plant is judged on either figure. The 10 kHz Q band is a
weak discriminator against linear interpolation and this section says so rather than moving the band after seeing
that: the real kernel clears 22.0 dB by about 7 dB (predicted Q 29.0 dB, gain -0.27 dB), and the linear plant clears
it by 0.3 dB.

### 4.2 The target ISA: samples and instructions on a big-endian MIPS guest (`D03-TARGET-RECEIPT`)

`toolchain/src/d03-target` is the resampler built for `mips-unknown-linux-musl` (soft-float, MIPS32r2, big-endian) and run
as PID 1 of the Debian 4.19 Malta guest under QEMU 11.1.2 with `-icount shift=0` (24Kf). `toolchain/src/measure-d03-target.py`
boots it and judges the serial transcript; `crates/franken-dsp/tests/d03_target_samples.rs` runs the same `cases.rs` on the
build host for the byte comparison. What the guest prints, and what the judge requires of it:

* **Samples.** The `f32` samples of the four +/-500 ppm quality cases (1 kHz and 10 kHz), as hex. The judge fits them with
  its own carrier fit against the **bands of section 4.1, declared before any Rust ran**, compares them sample by sample with
  the double-precision reference (limit 2e-6), and compares per-chunk digests with the host build: they must be
  **byte-identical**. A difference would be a finding about the target ISA's float behaviour, reported as such and not
  adjusted. Two further cases are seeded.
* **Replay defence.** The host packs a per-run seed into the initramfs; the guest derives two start phases from it
  (SplitMix64, mirrored bit for bit in the judge) and renders them. A transcript from another run, canned samples or samples
  copied from the host build cannot carry the right phase echo and samples (`D03T-SEEDED`). A truncated dump fails
  `D03T-TRANSCRIPT`. Both are planted in the receipt and must fail.
* **CPU.** Guest instructions per output frame of `DriftResampler::render` at 0 ppb, +/-500 ppm and mid-slew, on noise and
  on digital silence, read from the CP0 Count through a ruler calibrated in the same boot against loops of known length
  (before and after), after a settling sleep, with nothing printed until everything is measured; the ruler's shape
  agreement, before/after drift and linearity, and each measurement's floor, are gates.

**Recorded run** (`toolchain/results/runs/d84c5acebdc812c5c64a9af2feaa2d2c/`, ELF sha256 `7ae93f2f...`): two real boots
with different seeds pass all six gates; the planted replay fails `D03T-SEEDED` and the planted truncation fails
`D03T-TRANSCRIPT`. On the target: 1 kHz Q 91.707 dB (floor 85), gain -0.00003 dB; 10 kHz Q 28.977 dB (floor 22), gain
-0.2789 dB; all four dumps equal the host build's. Guest instructions per output frame (minimum of 5 repetitions of 2,048
frames): 0 ppb 2,283.7 on noise and 2,224.9 on silence; +500 ppm 3,040.0 and 2,685.5; -500 ppm 3,040.8 and 2,685.3; slewing
toward +500 ppm 3,044.1 (the second boot agrees within 0.15 %). At 48 kHz that is 107 to 146 million instructions per second
for one stereo stream. **0 ppb is not the worst case:** the fractional position is always zero there, and the soft-float
zero fast paths make it about 25 % cheaper on noise (17 % on silence) than the drift rows, which are the representative ones.

`D03-TARGET-RECEIPT` (in `scripts/e2e/d03`, every tier) re-judges every committed receipt of this kind: each stored
transcript must match its recorded sha256 and size, the host records their sha256, every re-derivable gate must equal the
receipt's, the real run must pass and both planted negatives must fail, and the recorded ELF must be proven soft-float
(0 FPU opcode words, `.MIPS.abiflags` soft). The ELF is not committed, so those two facts are recorded and not re-derived
here. With no receipt the case is blocked.

**Not covered by 4.2:** cycles on a real core (QEMU counts executed instructions; no cache, pipeline, memory-latency or
multiplier timing; no board clock, and the repository records none for any MIPS board, so no percentage of a CPU is
claimed); the PowerPC boards (hard-float, a different ISA, no run); the real DAC path; the 100 ppm, epoch-reset and slew
quality cases on the target (only the four +/-500 ppm cases ran there); programme material. The ruler's fitted intercept was
negative in both boots (about -1,270 and -700 ticks against regions of millions of instructions); its residual and drift
gates held, and the cause is not explained.

**Not covered, and why.** Cycles on a real core (needs hardware; section 4.2 has the instruction count and the ruler is
D02's). The figures above are host measurements of the emitted `f32` samples; section 4.2 repeats the four +/-500 ppm cases
on the big-endian MIPS target and finds the samples byte-identical (Rust does not contract multiply-adds). No PowerPC run
exists. Quality while the
ratio is slewing is not banded: a fixed carrier fit across a drifting frequency fails for the fit's own reasons.
Programme material, stereo cross-talk (the channels carry the same tone) and clipping (not reached at -1 dBFS) are not
measured.

## 5. Closed loop, and why a level trim exists

A rate estimate alone cannot bound the queue over hours: any residual rate error integrates into the level.
The glue policy (normative for users of these modules, exercised by `d03_closed_loop.rs`) is:

1. target = held estimator correction + `LevelTrim.trim_ppb(level)`, clamped by the resampler to its limit;
2. the held correction is the latest `Valid` or `Clamped` correction, held at most 60 s, then zero;
3. `Noisy` holds the previous correction; `RejoinRequired` means rebuffer, not resample;
4. an estimator discontinuity triggers `begin_epoch` on **both** the estimator and the resampler.

`LevelTrim` is proportional: 100 ppb per frame of level error, limited to 100 ppm. The queue excursion bound is
a first-order analysis, in `tests/d03/reference.py::occupancy_bound_frames`: warm-up drift
(`drift * warmup_frames`) + the slew triangle + (residual rate error / gain), times 3, plus 32. Without the loop
the same drift moves the queue by millions of frames in a day (negative control `D03-LOOP-UNCONTROLLED`).

## 8. Not covered, and why

* Everything above is host simulation. Nothing was run on a board. Section 4.2 is a QEMU run of the big-endian MIPS guest:
  samples and executed instructions, not hardware.
* Cycles on a real core: not measured (sections 4 and 4.2). Analog output, skew and audible quality are not measured.
* Quality is a worst-case bound for pure tones; programme material, a listening test, and any higher-order
  kernel are not evaluated.
* The legacy `BoundedDriftResampler` remains. Its removal, and any consumer migration, are outside this bead.

### Wiring

The estimator and the closed-loop test were held out of the build until the first green central compile,
because `franken-control` and `franken-speaker` depend on `franken-clock` and the tree is committed by a process
that snapshots it mid-edit. They are wired now: `franken-clock/src/lib.rs` declares `pub mod rate;`, and the
closed-loop test is at `crates/franken-speaker/tests/d03_closed_loop.rs` (a copy; `tests/d03/pending/` keeps the
original and is not committed). `franken-speaker` already depends on both crates, so no `Cargo.toml` or
`Cargo.lock` change was needed anywhere. The judge reads the final path first and the pending path as a fallback.

### Reading RCH logs

Through RCH the `Running` headers (stderr) and the test sections (stdout) are separate streams: in a real log the
sections can come first and the headers after, so a test cannot be attributed to the nearest preceding header
(the first judge did that and left 9 cases blocked on a green run). `run_cases.py` reuses D01's parser
(`tests/d01/run_cases.py::Logs`), which pairs the n-th header with the n-th section when the counts match, and
otherwise falls back to exact names with a name that appears twice staying ambiguous. D03 adds one rule:
`ignored` means "not run in this invocation" and yields to a real result from another log, so the soak test
(ignored in a normal run, `ok` under `--ignored`) is not a conflict. A missing or ambiguous test stays
`blocked`; `ok` against `FAILED`, or two different values for one record, fails. The self-test
