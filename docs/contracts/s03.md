# S03 contract: bounded compressed lookahead and seek/skip fencing (`ffw-mxt`)

Scope of this slice: the data structure and the rules in `crates/franken-session/src/lookahead.rs`. It is
placement-neutral: the same `LookaheadQueue` serves a member coordinator that decodes once (the
endpoint-native profile) or an optional host that supplies the same stream contract. Neither placement
closes the native-source task for the oldest audio profile, and nothing here fetches, decodes, renders,
authenticates or talks to a provider or a device.

## What is NOT claimed

* No Spotify, AirPlay, network, provider or physical-output behavior. No fetch duration is promised:
  `required_link_bps(bytes, window_ms)` is arithmetic (3 minutes at 320 kbit/s is 7,200,000 bytes and
  needs at least 28,800,000 bit/s to arrive in 2 s), not a measurement and not a service permission.
* S01 (session contract), S02 (negotiated codec profiles) and the decoded playout queue (N04) are not
  completed or replaced by this slice. The queue consumes facts they own (a chunk's exact decoded frame
  count and declared rate) and reports numbers they can surface (`LookaheadStats`).
* **The device adapter is UNQUALIFIED.** `crates/franken-speaker/src/output_fence.rs` implements
  `OutputFenceSource` over the render loop that owns a `franken-hal` session and retires each `Handoff`
  with the position the device accepted (see "The HAL output boundary" below). It reads numbers the fake
  device keeps; no real driver has been run, the HAL ABI carries no device sample rate (the output domain's
  rate is the render path's canonical rate), and a `Cancelled` retirement is the decoder owner's attestation.
  Any other fence source, and `UnqualifiedFence`, fails closed. A tail check cannot catch an adapter that
  reports a submission end smaller than what it really submitted: qualifying the adapter is the control for
  that.
* Qualification of a native source on any target profile, and the memory-ledger admission of the
  allowances, remain open.

## Accounting (state seconds and bytes separately; time is exact)

* `LookaheadLimits` holds three independent allowances: `max_bytes` (compressed payload), `max_media_ms`
  (media time) and `max_chunks`. A full queue says which one bound (`Full { cap }`); a push exactly at an
  allowance is accepted and one unit over is refused.
* A chunk's duration is its exact decoded frame count (`frames`) at its declared `rate_hz`. Positions,
  contiguity and seek targets are exact frame numbers within a track, so no rounded millisecond ever
  accumulates into a position. Only the time cap and the derived media time use nanoseconds, rounded UP per
  chunk by `duration_ns_ceil`; the same value is added on admission and removed on hand-out or eviction, so
  there is no drift and the cap is never under-counted (the over-count is below 1 ns per chunk).
* All chunks of a track declare one rate (`RateMismatch` otherwise); the next track may declare its own.
* There is **no bit-rate window**. Variable-bit-rate streams, bursts, a preroll and header or
  codec-configuration chunks of ZERO frames (bytes, no time, no movement) are valid negotiated content and
  are judged only by the byte, time and chunk caps. Authoritative codec facts (frame counts, rate) come from
  the codec side (S02); this queue does not invent min/max bit rates to second-guess them.
* Readiness is a measured minimum of media time (`ready_min_media_ms`), not a full fill, and it is false
  while a transition is unfenced. Startup never waits for the whole allowance.
* The queue owns exactly the bytes it charges: an accepted payload is stored with capacity equal to its
  length, so a `Vec` that reserved far more cannot keep its reservation behind a small byte count. (This
  is the queue's own allocation, separate from later allocator-overhead and RSS qualification.)
* Compressed lookahead is separate from the decoded playout queue; this module never touches playout.

## Identity and the gapless fetch/playback distinction

* Every chunk carries the source epoch (`SourceEpoch`, minted by the arbiter) and an opaque `TrackSerial`
  the adapter assigns. A track URI or account identifier never enters the queue.
* A chunk of an older epoch is refused and counted (`LateOldEpoch`); a future epoch is refused
  (`EpochAhead`).
* `fetch_position()` is the ADMISSION position: the track and frame the next chunk must start at. It
  advances to the next track (frame 0) only after a chunk marked `last_of_track` is admitted.
  `head_track()` (front of the queue) and `playback_track()` (last handed out) are separate. A gapless
  queue holds the old track's tail and the next track's head at the same time; admitting the last chunk of
  a track moves only the fetch position, never what is queued, and the tail is still handed out after the
  next head was admitted.

## Eviction (explicit, furthest first, counts only)

* A push into a full queue back-pressures; nothing is dropped.
* When the memory ledger shrinks the allowance, `shrink_to` drops the chunks furthest in the future first
  (possibly all of them), moves `fetch_position()` back to the first dropped chunk (restoring track, frame
  and rate) so the fetcher refetches contiguously, and reports counts. A queued chunk larger than a
  reduced `max_chunk_bytes` goes too, together with everything admitted after it (the queue is contiguous),
  wherever it sits, including at the head. The module has no I/O and never formats a payload, so an
  eviction cannot write a secret.

## Handoffs: decoder work the device cannot see

* `pop` returns a `Handoff` (the chunk and an opaque `HandoffTicket`). The ticket stays outstanding until
  the adapter calls `retire`: `Submitted { end }` with the OUTPUT position where its decoded audio ends, or
  `Cancelled` (decode cancelled and joined). Outstanding tickets are bounded by `max_outstanding`
  (`OutstandingLimit`; the refused chunk stays queued).
* An output position is a frame count in an explicit `OutputDomain` (device session, generation, rate).
  Counts from two domains never compare. One epoch's submissions live in one domain (`OutputDomainChanged`
  otherwise), and the queue records the largest end seen.
* A retired or unknown ticket may not submit (`may_submit`, `UnknownHandoff`), so a decoder that ignored a
  cancellation must drop what it produced. A submission that retires after a transition began is still
  counted: a device measurement taken before it cannot cover it.
* A ticket belongs to the queue that issued it. It carries a clone of that queue's identity (an `Arc`
  compared by pointer: no counter, no 64-bit atomic, no randomness; the clone also keeps the allocation
  alive, so a dropped queue's address is never reused under a live ticket). A ticket of another queue,
  even with the same epoch and sequence number, is `ForeignHandoff`: it retires nothing, authorizes
  nothing and reduces no other queue's outstanding work.

## Seek and skip

1. `begin_transition(epoch, track, target_frame)` requires an epoch strictly ahead, drops every buffered
   chunk of the old timeline at once, and opens ingress for the new epoch and track.
2. If any chunk of the old timeline was handed out and not yet fenced, the result carries a `FenceRequest`
   (a fresh token and the epoch whose output must be accounted for) and the seek is NOT acknowledged:
   `pop` stays refused and readiness is false. If nothing was handed out, the seek is complete at once.
3. `ack_transition(&mut renderer)` does not take a verdict. First every handed-out chunk must have
   retired (see Handoffs): while decoder work is outstanding the device cannot see it, so an empty device
   proves nothing, and the renderer is not even asked (`OutstandingDecoderWork`). Then it asks an
   `OutputFenceSource` for evidence about the pending request, once, and accepts only a report that echoes
   the request's token, speaks for the epoch whose output was handed out, is in the SAME output domain as
   the recorded submissions, has `tail >= the last submitted end` (`FenceTailBehindSubmissions`
   otherwise), and shows `Drained { tail, played }` with `played >= tail` or
   `FlushAcknowledged { domain, waited_ticks }`. The renderer is not consulted when no transition is
   pending or it already failed. Refusing the old epoch's ingress alone is never an acknowledgment.
4. `FlushTimedOut { waited_ticks }` fails the transition for good: no later report rescues it and `pop`
   stays refused. A later `begin_transition` supersedes a pending or failed one and is fenced against the
   SAME original output epoch, with a new token, so a reply for the superseded request is stale.

The evidence has the shape of `franken-hal`: `irrevocable_until_frames()` is the tail, the output cursor is
the played frame, and `DrainOutcome::{Acknowledged, TimedOut}` are the other two outcomes, each in the
device session's domain; the group readiness report carries the same facts as
`old_output_tail_complete_frame` and `can_stop_flush`.

## The HAL output boundary (`crates/franken-speaker/src/output_fence.rs`)

`OutputBoundary` sits between one `LookaheadQueue` and the `RenderLoop` that owns the device session. It
passes retirements to the queue and records them only when the queue accepted them; `HalFence` (from
`OutputBoundary::fence_over`) is the `OutputFenceSource` for one `ack_transition`. It measures when asked.

* **Output domain** = `HalSession::session_id()` (unique in the process, never reused), `HalSession::generation()`
  (flushes and resets the device acknowledged; the frame counters keep counting across one, so this is what
  separates the audio before it from the audio after) and the canonical sample rate (the one rate the render
  path produces; the HAL ABI carries none). A chunk at 44.1 kHz and an output domain at 48 kHz are different
  domains: a position labelled with the chunk's rate is refused by the queue.
* **End of a handoff's audio** = `irrevocable_until_frames()` read after the renderer submitted it, never
  arithmetic on chunk frames. `retire_submitted` is refused while a render block is in progress (the loop's
  own `block_in_progress`, which a cancel, a fault or a recovery that drops a block clears), while the
  session is not running or draining, while the caller reports decoded frames the renderer has not pulled,
  and for a ticket the queue does not accept (foreign, unknown, already retired, or cancelled).
* **Drained** = the epoch's own recorded end as `tail`, the consumed count less the stated position error as
  `played` (zero when the precision is unknown or the counter went backwards). Asked before the audio is
  played it reports the true numbers and the queue refuses them with nothing changed.
* **Flush acknowledged** = the session's generation is past the generation the audio was submitted in: the
  device acknowledged a flush or a reset after that audio. That is a fact about the old audio whatever the
  loop has done since (it may have started again), and the report names the OLD domain. A stop the device
  never acknowledged is a fault and is reported as a flush that timed out, which fails the transition for
  good; an acknowledged reset plus a new transition recovers it.
* **Cancelled** is the decoder owner's attestation that nothing of the handoff is or will be in the output.
  An epoch whose handoffs all retired as cancelled has nothing to drain. A boundary that never saw the
  retirements has no evidence and reports a flush that never acknowledged.
* The boundary does not drain, flush, stop or acknowledge anything itself, and it is not a second renderer.

## Evidence and status

* Rust tests: `crates/franken-session/src/lookahead/tests.rs` (scenarios for each rule above, each refusal
  paired with its permitted counterpart, plus a seeded schedule that checks every invariant after every
  operation and counts each outcome class). `crates/franken-speaker/src/output_fence/tests.rs` (16 tests over
  a real `RenderLoop` and `HalSession` on the fake device), three tests in `franken-hal` (`session_id` and
  `generation`) and one in `render.rs` (`block_in_progress`, which a cancel or a fault clears).
* The same scenarios, the same SplitMix64 schedule and a mutation matrix of 58 broken rules run in an
  independent Python mirror. That is model evidence of the algorithm; it does not verify the Rust
  transcription. The Rust is NOT compiled by its author: formatting, strict Clippy and the tests are
  central activities.
