## 1. What the carrier is

`CarrierSender` serves any number of peers and `CarrierReceiver` serves one; each owns a **port** (`DatagramPort`: two
non-blocking sockets, media and control, with `send_to`, `recv`, `local_addr` and an all-or-nothing `rebind`). The carrier is
generic over the port: `HostUdp` (two `std` UDP sockets, for host tests) and the speaker's `SocketPort` over the P02 boundary (§9)
run the same bounded logic. Both are driven by the caller (`pump`, `poll`, `poll_into`): nothing blocks, nothing reads a clock
(the caller passes `now_ns`), and the work per call is capped.

* **Loopback only.** `CarrierLimits::loopback_only` (true in the baseline) refuses to bind or admit any address that is not
  loopback, and `allow_ipv6` (false) refuses an address of an undeclared family, both before a socket is used. An address
  family a profile does not declare is never silently routed.
* **Frames are queued, datagrams are signed at send time.** A queued item is an `OutboundFrame` (a block of up to 256 stereo
  frames); each peer has its own `StreamSender` (N01), so what a peer receives has dense sequence numbers whatever was dropped
  on the way, and the receiver's reorder and future-gap windows are never spent on packets the sender itself discarded.
* **Per-peer and aggregate bounds.** A peer queue is capped in packets and bytes (wire bytes: header, payload, tag). All peer
  queues together are capped too. When the aggregate cap is hit, the oldest frame of the *longest* queue is dropped, not the
  newcomer's, so a slow peer cannot take the room of the others. Control has its own per-peer queue; media and control never
  evict each other.
* **Pacing.** One token bucket per interface, in nano-bytes, starts full (`pace_burst_bytes`), refills at
  `pace_bytes_per_sec`, and is charged each datagram's wire size plus 28 (IPv4) or 48 (IPv6) header bytes. Peers are served
  round robin, one datagram per turn. When the bucket cannot afford the next datagram the pump says when it can
  (`next_send_ns`, rounded up to the nanosecond). A refill credits at most 4 s of elapsed time.
* **Groups: several streams on one interface.** One `CarrierSender` owns one interface and may carry several streams, each a
  *group* with its own key, stream identity and epoch and its own peers (`add_group`, `add_peer_in_group`, `submit_group_media`,
  `advance_group_epoch`, `remove_group`; the plain calls mean the primary group the sender is built with, which is never
  removed). Every group's peers sit in the one peer list, so the interface bucket, control's share of it, the aggregate packet
  and byte caps (the longest queue pays, in whichever group it is), `max_sends_per_pump` and the admission sum
  `(peers + 1) * peer_load <= pace_bytes_per_sec` are the interface's and are checked across all groups (`N07-U02`). **Fairness is
  per peer, not per group:** peers are served round robin, so a group with seven peers takes seven eighths of a saturated bucket
  against a group with one. Groups are bounded by `max_peers`, and a peer id names one peer on the interface (a speaker in two
  groups is two peers). A report is checked with the key of the group whose stream identity it names (the identity is read
  before the tag and only chooses the key); a report that names no group is checked with the primary key, as it always was; a
  peer id that is not a member of the named group is an unknown source and credits nothing. A second `CarrierSender` over the
  same interface would have its own bucket, caps and admission sum, which is why groups live in one sender. **Repair traffic is
  outside this claim:** a paced N06 sender would have to submit its datagrams through this sender to be charged to the same
  bucket, and that lane does not exist. Its wire is 1152 bytes with 128 bytes of overhead (112 header, 16 tag), where this
  carrier's cached-datagram size, queue validation and path-MTU admission are written for N01's 1104 and 80, so the lane needs
  its own accounting before it can be claimed.
* **Source restart.** `restart_group(group, new_stream_id, new_epoch)` takes **a fresh stream identity and a strictly newer
  epoch**, both announced by the control plane (the carrier makes up neither, and there is no same-epoch restart); the identity
  must differ from every group's on the interface. The group's peers drop what they had queued, held or retained (counted as
  fenced), number again from 0 under the new stream, and keep their addresses, paths and an MTU fence; a report that names the
  old stream is a wrong stream, a submit under the old epoch is stale, and no other group is touched. The key is the group's, so a
  rekey is a new group. The receiver follows with `CarrierReceiver::restart_stream(stream_id, epoch)` (N01's `bind_stream`: the
  replay window resets, the old stream is refused as a wrong stream, reports name the new stream from position 0, the backlog is
  dropped). In a speaker, `MediaEndpoint::restart_stream` makes both calls together, the media path's `MediaPlayout::bind_stream`
  (an N04/N06 owner's method) and then the carrier's, so no poll sees one without the other; the control path still makes the
  clock's calls (`begin_epoch`, `admit_start`), and under N06 a fresh `enable_host_fec` for the new stream is the owner's call and
  is not exercised (`N07-E22` is N01 mode).
* **Deadlines.** A frame whose own `consume_by_ns` has passed is dropped at the next pump and counted as late bytes: audio
  that could only be heard late is not sent, and a peer that recovers from a stall is sent fresh audio only. **The one exception
  is not audio and exists for recovery:** the datagram that holds the last allowed sequence number (below) and a probe, which
  sends the newest transmitted datagram again, may go after their `consume_by`. They are authentic; the receiver's engine calls
  them `Late` and never plays them; what they earn is the receiver's authenticated report. That this renders no stale PCM is
  shown through the actual `MediaEndpoint`, N04 engine and platform clock (`N07-E19`..`N07-E21`, `N07-E10`), not through the
  bare carrier receiver.
* **Windows and reports (receiver backpressure).** A receiver sends an authenticated report of the next sequence it has
  *consumed* (taken from its bounded inbound queue), on its own schedule and whether or not new media has arrived. The sender's
  window counts the datagrams it **transmitted** and that are not yet reported (`in_flight`, at most `window_packets`, itself
  capped at 4096 = `MAX_WINDOW_PACKETS`; in effect never above the sequence budget below): a datagram the transport refused never left, so it holds no place, and a report's gaps
  are fine because a report covers every transmitted number below it. A sequence number is **allocated when a frame is signed and
  is never reused**; no acknowledgement is ever invented (an address change does not forgive the datagrams sent to the old
  path). A peer at the limit pauses; its queue fills, ages and drops its own frames; the other peers are served as before.
  Reports are authenticated with the stream key and a context of their own (§3), must name the current stream and epoch, must
  come from the peer's registered control address, and may not claim a sequence the sender has not allocated.
* **The sequence budget.** N01 admits a first packet at most 1024 beyond origin 0 and a later one at most 1024 beyond the highest
  number admitted (`MAX_FORWARD_SEQ_GAP`; that fence is not touched here), and a receiver's highest admitted number is at least
  `reported - 1`. A sender that allocated numbers without a bound while a transport refused for long enough would therefore end
  up beyond the receiver's reach, with every later datagram refused as a future sequence and no report ever coming back to move
  the base. So **no number is allocated 1000 or more beyond the report** (`SEQ_AHEAD_LIMIT` = 1000: 24 below the fence, and above
  the largest window, which a compile-time assertion keeps true). When all 1000 are allocated and nothing is held, the peer is
  *sequence-blocked* (`CarrierSender::sequence_blocked(id)`, a typed state for the control plane) and nothing new is signed.
* **The datagram that holds the last number is protected.** The last number the receiver is certain to admit is held by a
  datagram that is **never dropped, aged out, evicted or purged by an MTU fence**, and is tried again every `STALL_PROBE_NS`
  (250 ms) for as long as the transport refuses it; a kernel "too long" for it fences the path but keeps the datagram, and the
  same bytes go when the path carries them again. When it goes and a genuine receiver admits it, that receiver's own report moves
  the base and the budget reopens (tested against the real N01 receiver, including the negative control that the same receiver
  refuses number 1025).
* **Probes.** A peer that is *waiting* (its window is full, or its sequence budget is spent) and has audio queued is sent **one
  probe per `STALL_PROBE_NS`**: its newest transmitted datagram again, byte for byte and under the same number. A probe allocates
  no number, holds no place in the window, is charged to the interface bucket, and is counted apart from media
  (`ControlStats::probes_sent`, `probe_bytes`). A receiver that never saw it admits it (its number is within the fence), consumes
  it, and its report covers the window; a receiver that already has it refuses the copy as a replay, and its next periodic report
  carries the position. A peer that never answers costs one datagram per interval for as long as it is silent, with no number
  burned (tested over 1,100 intervals).
* **A refused send.** The signed datagram is held whole and tried again at the next pump with the same bytes and number
  (`SEND_RETRY_ATTEMPTS` = 3), nothing behind it signed meanwhile, then given up on: one number is spent, and the peer is left
  alone for an interval, so a transport that keeps refusing spends numbers at about four a second until the budget above stops it
  (and not one per block). "Too long" (`EMSGSIZE`) is not retried: the path is fenced. **A held datagram is part of every queue
  bound**: it counts in its peer's packet and byte caps and in the aggregate caps, it is never the one evicted to make room, and
  when a peer holds only that datagram and is at its cap, the newcomer is what is refused (and counted). It ages out by its own
  `consume_by`, except the protected datagram above.
* **What this does not do.** A peer whose receiver never answers stays *sequence-blocked*, or waiting, for as long as it is silent:
  rejoining it (a receiver that restarted and lost its replay window, or one whose reports can no longer be trusted) is the
  control plane's **epoch transition** (`advance_epoch` on both sides, which restarts numbering from zero), and nothing in this
  carrier performs it by itself. That is a stated limitation, not a recovery claim.
* **Retained bytes.** The queue caps count the queue and the held datagram. The sender also keeps, per peer, the newest transmitted
  datagram whole (`last_sent`, at most one `MAX_DATAGRAM_BYTES`, so that a probe can send it again), the list of transmitted-and-
  unreported numbers (8 bytes each, at most the smaller of the window and the sequence budget) and the queued control datagrams.
  None of those is inside the aggregate byte cap, so that cap alone does not bound what is retained: with eight peers at their
  caps the cached datagrams alone add 8,832 B. `CarrierSender::retained_bytes()` reports every kind **by what is allocated
  (`capacity`), not by what is logically in use**, and `CarrierLimits::max_retained_bytes()` is the closed-form bound (queue per
  the caps, `max_peers` cached datagrams, the list at its bound, full control queues). The unreported list is allocated once, at
  its bound (the smaller of the window and the sequence budget, 8 bytes a slot), when a peer is admitted, and keeps that
  allocation until the peer leaves, so a report, an epoch restart or a move that empties it changes the report by nothing (tested:
  fill, clear by report, move, epoch restart). The byte vectors are built to their exact length, so for them capacity is length
  (tested). The slot arrays of the queues are struct-sized and are reported apart as `queue_slots` (a count) and left out of the
  total, as are counters, keys, fixed fields, the allocator's overhead, kernel buffers and resident set. The **peer table** (one
  slot of fixed per-peer state for each of `max_peers`) is allocated once, at that size, when the sender is built, and admitting a
  peer fills a slot without allocating; `CarrierSender::peer_table_bytes()` reports it by capacity, apart from the payload and
  sequence kinds above, and `CarrierLimits::max_peer_table_bytes()` is its bound, so the slots no peer fills stay allocated and
  stay counted. **No CPU or memory figure is measured**; the bound is arithmetic over the limits, not a resident-set claim.
* **One bound on a pump's work.** `max_sends_per_pump` bounds every attempt on the media lane in one pump: a datagram that left,
  a send the transport refused, and a probe all count, so a number of stalled or refused peers cannot make one pump do more
  work than the cap. `PumpReport.media_sent` still counts only datagrams that left, and probes are counted apart
  (`ControlStats.probes_sent`), so the submitted-media books are unchanged. The peer the cap stopped is served first by the
  next pump (the round-robin cursor has moved past the peers already served).
* **Control first, charged, and media not starved.** Each pump sends up to `control_per_pump` control datagrams (opaque bytes of
  at most 512, authenticated by the layer above) before any media, but only what two budgets afford: the **interface bucket**
  (every control datagram is charged its length plus the IP and UDP headers to the same bucket as media, so control counts
  against the aggregate cap however often the caller pumps) and **control's own share** of it (a second bucket refilled at 1/8 of
  the interface rate with a burst of `control_per_pump` largest control datagrams). What is not affordable stays in its bounded
  queue. Because control is served first in each pump, media saturation cannot starve a stop (it waits at most for the refill of
  its own cost); because control is limited to its share, a control flood cannot starve media.
* **A move is checked against the path.** `peer_address_changed` re-checks the peer's path MTU for the new address family before
  anything changes: IPv6 carries 20 bytes less per datagram than IPv4, so a peer admitted at 1140 over IPv4 (a block fits) cannot
  move to IPv6 (it would not) and is refused with `PATH_MTU_TOO_SMALL`, as at admission; a peer whose path carries the block
  moves. The caller's word on the MTU (`set_path_mtu`) is what lifts the refusal.
* **Epoch fence.** `submit_media` takes the epoch the caller was authorised under and refuses any other (`STALE_EPOCH`).
  `advance_epoch` drops everything queued under the old epoch (counted as fenced bytes, never sent), restarts every peer's
  sequence and consumed position at zero, and makes reports of the old epoch inert. A receiver that has not adopted the new
  epoch rejects its packets (N01 `FUTURE_EPOCH`) and is rejected in turn (its reports are stale).
* **MTU.** The largest payload a path carries is `(path MTU - IP/UDP headers - 64 - 16)` rounded down to whole frames, at most
  1024 (`max_payload_for_path_mtu`). An N01 block cannot be split (start frames are 256-aligned), so a peer whose path cannot
  carry `required_payload_bytes` is refused at admission, or fenced if its MTU drops later: its queue is dropped, nothing is
  sent to it, nothing is fragmented, the other peers are untouched, and it resumes when its path recovers.
* **Admission.** `add_peer` checks, in order, a duplicate id, the peer count, both addresses, the path MTU and the pacing
  budget (`(peers + 1) * peer_load_bytes_per_sec <= pace_bytes_per_sec`); a refusal leaves the peers already carried exactly as
  they were. The budget is a configured estimate, not a measurement.
* **Simulated events.** `set_link_up(false)` makes pumps send and read nothing; queues stay bounded and age out. `peer_address_changed`
  keeps a peer's identity, sequence and counters and moves its addresses; reports from the old control address are refused.
  Neither touches an interface.

Per peer, `media_submitted = media_sent + dropped (overflow, aggregate, late, fenced, send error) + queued` holds in packets
and in wire bytes at every call boundary; the judge checks it on every observed record.

## 3. The report (receiver to sender, control socket)

48 bytes: `FFWR` magic, version `1`, **a 24-bit peer id** (it was three reserved zero bytes in the first slice; 0 means
unattributed), `stream_id` (u64 BE), `epoch` (u64 BE), `consumed_next` (u64 BE) and a 16-byte tag, the first 16 bytes of
`HMAC-SHA256(key, "FFWR-v1-auth" || body)` where the body is the first 32 bytes (so the peer id is covered by the tag). Structure
is checked before the MAC, then the stream, the epoch, the peer and the claim, each counted in its own bucket (`reports_malformed`,
`reports_bad_tag`, `reports_wrong_stream`, `reports_stale_epoch`, `reports_unknown_source`, `reports_invalid`).

**Attribution.** A report that names a peer is attributed by that number and, when the port can say where the datagram came from
(the P02 `recvfrom` boundary does), only if it came from that peer's registered control address; a report that names no peer needs
the source address and is refused if the port has none. Every receiver of a stream holds the stream key, so the peer id says which
*holder of the key* spoke, not which receiver: the same trust the media rests on. A peer id must fit three bytes
(`CarrierError::PeerIdTooLarge`).

The worlds `N07-E01` to `N07-E07` are scripts in `crates/franken-stream/tests/vectors/n07_scenarios.json`, generated by
`python3 tests/n07/model.py --write`. The Rust test is a small interpreter that plays a script over real loopback sockets
(a real `CarrierSender`, one real `CarrierReceiver` per peer, and a real relay socket where a script asks for loss), waits for
exactly the datagrams each step caused (so the run is lock-step, with a bounded timeout and no sleeping otherwise), compares
every received block with an independently written sample formula, prints the record it observed as `N07-OBSERVED`, and fails
if that record differs from the model's. `run_cases.py` then recomputes the model's record from the script, not from the file,
and compares it with the printed one key for key. The model and the Rust are two statements of the same rules; they share no code.

**What is actual and what is simulated.** Actual: the sockets, the datagrams, their bytes and checks (N01), the queues, the pacer,
the windows, the epoch fence, the loss and reorder of the relay hop. Simulated: the clock (a script's), the interface (a flag),
the address change (a new pair of sockets for one peer), the unreliable hop (a seeded relay on loopback). Not measured: CPU,
memory, real loss, jitter or radio behaviour. The model assumes that loopback delivers in order and loses nothing between the
carrier and a receiver (Linux and macOS loopback do); a host that does otherwise turns a case red, which is the outcome wanted.

## 8. The retained-network lifecycle and the ownership matrix (`franken-stream/src/link.rs`)

`LinkManager` is a pure state machine (the caller passes the time and each event and performs the returned actions):
`Down`, `WaitingForAddress`, `Binding`, `Up`, `Backoff`, `Failed`. Events: `InterfaceUp/Down`, `AddressBound{ip, lease}`,
`AddressLost`, `LeaseRenewed`, `RebindResult{ip, ok}`, and `ResolverChanged` / `WanReachability`, which are counted and change
nothing. Actions: `Quiesce{reason}`, `Rebind{ip, attempt}`, `ReleaseStale{old_ip}`, `Resume{ip, generation}`, `GiveUp{reason}`.

* **Bounded reconnect.** A failed rebind backs off `initial * 2^(n-1)` capped at `max`, plus seeded jitter of at most a quarter
  step (so a mixed fleet does not retry in step and a run replays from its seed); it never decreases within an outage; after
  `max_rebind_attempts` the manager reports `GiveUp{ReconnectBudgetSpent}` once and waits for the next connectivity event.
* **Address families are declared per profile** (`LinkProfile`); all three tracked profiles declare IPv4 only. An address of an
  undeclared family is refused and never replaces a working path; with nothing else on offer the bounded wait ends in
  `GiveUp{UnsupportedAddressFamily}`.
* **Lease guard.** Sending stops one guard before a lease ends unless it was renewed; a lapsed lease is no address. The lease is
  checked before **every** event and tick, in every state: an interface that was down across the lease's end is not resumed onto
  that address, a rebind that finishes after it is stale, and a backoff running when it ends is cancelled. A short outage inside
  the lease resumes.
* **Stale paths.** A rebind result for an address that moved on is ignored; an address that disappears and returns is not
  trusted to have kept its sockets and is rebound; `ReleaseStale` names the old address once.
* **Identity.** `PeerDirectory` maps a stable speaker id to its peer handle and current path; a speaker keeps both across an
  address change, an older path generation never overwrites a newer one, another speaker cannot take its address or handle, and
  the directory is bounded.
* **UX01.** `TransportReason` has stable codes and a sentence naming the local condition; none mentions the WAN
  (`net_carrier::readiness_for` maps each to `DegradedStandaloneRecovery`, never `FaultedSafeMuted`).

**Ownership matrix** (`link::OWNERSHIP`, 21 duties). A duty is `Rust` only where this tree performs it, with a test
(`socket.rebind`, `path.stale_cleanup`, `identity.stable_across_ip_change`, `reconnect.backoff`, `addr.lease_watch`,
`transport.failure_reasons`, `wan.decoupling`; `link.state_events` and `p02.datagram_send` are `Rust` policy with a named gap). Association,
address acquisition, lease renewal, routes and resolvers are `Unestablished`: nobody has shown who performs them in the stock
system or whether they survive replacing the daemon, and nothing here assumes either answer (a missing capability is explicit,
not a retained one). The seven E04 radio duties (`radio.multicast_delivery`, `radio.unicast_delivery`, `radio.power_save`,
`radio.roam`, `radio.congestion`, `radio.mesh_membership`, `radio.scheduler_interference`) carry E04's record ids and both
blockers (`EXT-E04-RADIO-OWNERSHIP`, `EXT-E04-LIVE-QUAL`) verbatim; `tests/n07_ownership.rs` reads the committed
`tests/e04/matrix_audiodev_m16.json` and fails, with a message that says so, the day E04 establishes one of them.
**Wireless is tracked as required and unqualified**: every `LinkProfile` names the external receipt that would qualify it
(`Q03`, `Q03+Q06`) and no field can say "qualified".

## 9. The speaker wiring (`franken-speaker/src/net_carrier.rs`)

* **P02.** `SocketPort` is a `DatagramPort` built from `socket::open_datagram_socket`, `bind_v4`/`bind_v6` and the platform's
  `datagram::{send_datagram_to, recv_datagram_from}`. It binds **configured** ports (there is no `getsockname`), sends with
  `sendto` from the bound socket so the source port is the advertised one, and learns each datagram's sender. A cut or
  undecodable sender is `PortFault::Discarded` (the kernel consumed the datagram: counted, never decoded, reading goes on). The
  errno classification is the ABI's own (`Abi::classify_net_errno`): `EMSGSIZE` fences the peer, `EADDRNOTAVAIL`/`ENETUNREACH`
  are a path problem. A rebind to the address already bound is a no-op; otherwise it opens both lanes on the new address and
  the same ports and closes the old ones only when both opened.
* **N01.** Frames are signed at send time per peer; the receiving side hands each raw datagram to `MediaPlayout::ingest` (one
  admission, in the order that module documents: the clock before the replay window), through `CarrierReceiver::poll_into` and a
  `MediaSink`. An authentic datagram is consumed whatever the engine then decides, so a stale stream still advances its window; a
  platform-clock failure is backpressure and earns no credit.
* **N04.** `MediaEndpoint::prepare` is `MediaPlayout::prepare`: PCM prepared for a scheduled sink while the group clock allows it.
  Preparing is not playing; nothing here drives a DAC. After an outage the engine itself catches up muted (bounded concealment,
  then silence, then fresh blocks only); the carrier never replays queued audio.
* **Link.** `SenderEndpoint` applies the manager's actions to the carrier and keeps the directory and the carrier's peers in step
  (a transaction on a clone of the directory, so a refusal changes neither). The carrier's gate starts where the manager does
  (down): nothing is sent before the manager has said the link is up.

## 10. What the speaker worlds are, and are not

The worlds (`N07-E11` to `N07-E21`) run the real `CarrierSender`/`CarrierReceiver`, the real N01 framers, the real `LinkManager`
and the real `MediaPlayout` and `GroupClock` over **real loopback UDP sockets behind a simulated host kernel** (`HostKernel`),
which implements the platform's `DatagramOps` over `std` sockets and answers `Provenance::Simulated`. It checks that `socket`
arrives with the datagram type and `SOCK_NONBLOCK|SOCK_CLOEXEC`, `sendto` with flags 0 and `recvfrom` with `MSG_TRUNC`. **It is
host-only evidence**: it says nothing about the Linux leaf, `socketcall` on either ABI (no guest has issued one; this would be
the first on MIPS), a legacy kernel, an interface, a lease server or a radio. The oracles are independent of the code under test:
the source block is a pure function of its index (every prepared sample is compared with it, and each real block must equal
exactly one source block), the expected drops and fences come from the schedule or from what was queued, and the books
(`submitted = sent + dropped + queued`, in packets and wire bytes; the sink and N01 receiver arithmetic) are checked by the judge
from the printed record. Each world prints `N07-OBSERVED` with its seed, fault schedule, link log, denominators, dropped and late
bytes, pacing delay and failure counters. CPU and memory are not measured. Link events are synthetic.
